# Trump-era cater-mode discount

In the current political environment (2025-2030), GOP candidates face strong pressure to align with Trump and the MAGA base. Public positions that would otherwise read as institutional violations may be rhetorical concession to that pressure rather than a reflection of personal belief. Apply a scoring discount on the institutionalist axis instead of hard-filtering — but distinguish carefully between rhetoric and action.

## Triggering case

A philosophy with hard filters for *"promotes 2020-election-fraud claims"* and *"attempted to seize ballots or interfere with election machinery"* would, in CA-2026, eliminate the entire viable GOP field (Hilton and Bianco) before any analysis. Relaxing those hard filters to a scoring *discount* — on the reasoning that a GOP candidate may voice such positions as political survival rather than personalist conviction — extends the more general *"tolerate pragmatic cater to Trump"* principle while still penalizing the behavior.

## How to apply

Tier the discount by how **performative vs. action-based** the institutional concession is:

| Concession type | Institutionalist score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Rhetorical questioning of 2020 (TV commentary, social media, debate-stage repetition) | −1 | Performative cater-mode plausible; many GOP candidates do this to avoid being primaried |
| Refusal to enforce specific laws/orders (selective, narrow scope) | −1 to −2 | Depends on legality and scope — e.g. a sheriff's COVID-order refusal is mid-tier |
| Active executive action interfering with elections (actual ballot seizure, vote-certification challenge, voter-roll purges beyond procedure) | **−4** (institutionalist scale extends negative for this category) | Ballot seizure / machinery interference warrants a score beyond the normal ±2 scale — effectively keeps the candidate out of the viable pool while preserving the scoring framework rather than hard-filtering |
| Personalist endorsement of strongman politics (advocating norm-violation as policy, modeling Trump-style demagoguery) | Hard filter — survives the Trump-era discount | This is the line. Active personalist mode, not pragmatic cater |

## When it applies

- GOP candidates in races 2025-2030
- Specifically the institutionalist axis (Tier 2)
- Does NOT apply outside the Trump era (revisit post-2030)

## Don't confuse with

- **Anti-personalist-strongman** (Tier 1, character) — that's about the candidate's own character, separate from cater-mode analysis. A candidate who has built their identity around Trump-personalism still scores poorly on Tier 1 regardless of the Tier 2 cater-discount.
- **[[establishment-career-not-equals-institutionalist]]** — that calibration is about over-*crediting* on institutionalist; this one is about over-*debiting* on a specific category of concession.

## Scale extension

The general per-axis scoring scale in [[../SKILL.md]] is ±2. The institutionalist axis is an exception: it extends to **−4** for action-tier executive interference with elections. Rationale: hard-filter and standard scoring are both inadequate for this category. Hard filter is too binary (eliminates candidates who might be tolerable under cater-mode framing); standard −2 is too compressed (lumps active ballot seizure with rhetorical fraud-questioning). The −4 tier preserves scoring continuity while making the candidate functionally non-competitive.

Future axes may need similar asymmetric extension as precedent emerges. Don't extend speculatively — wait for a triggering case like this one.

## Worked example

An active ballot-seizure attempt scores **−4** (action tier) — functionally out of the viable pool, but scored rather than hard-filtered. Mere rhetorical questioning of 2020 results scores **−1** (rhetorical tier). Whether *promotion* of fraud claims (vs. mere questioning) should tighten the rhetorical tier toward −2 is a per-cycle judgment the user makes; this skill provides the tiers, the user's philosophy provides the threshold.

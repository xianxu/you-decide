# Daniel M. Chung — 2026 Santa Clara County District Attorney candidate

## Background

Daniel M. Chung, 37, grew up in Milpitas. BA from Harvard; JD from Columbia Law School. Started his prosecution career in the Bronx, handling gun crimes. In 2018 Jeff Rosen hired him into the Santa Clara County DA's office as a Deputy District Attorney, where he has worked violent-crime and felony cases. [(Palo Alto Daily Post, May 2026)](https://padailypost.com/2026/05/13/personal-feud-colors-race-for-da/)

He ran against Rosen in 2022, finishing second in a three-way race (79,378 votes vs. Rosen's 181,851). He is running again in 2026, making this his second consecutive internal challenge to his own boss. [(Palo Alto Daily Post)](https://padailypost.com/2026/05/13/personal-feud-colors-race-for-da/)

**Current employment status:** Chung is officially on paid administrative leave — collecting an $8,525 biweekly paycheck — while running full-time against Rosen. [(Palo Alto Daily Post, Apr 2026)](https://padailypost.com/2026/04/13/da-sued-for-paying-prosecutor-not-to-work/)

## Internal-challenger signal

Chung being an internal challenger is a meaningful datum. Running against your own employer is a significant professional and financial risk that career opportunists don't take twice. His motivations are consistent across time: he publicly criticized Rosen's progressive-prosecution posture as early as February 2021 (an op-ed on "progressive prosecutors" after a wave of anti-Asian hate crimes) before any campaign was in motion. The subsequent firings and reinstatements — two of them — intensified a personal feud that now runs alongside the policy disagreement, which muddies the signal somewhat. The feud is real and acknowledged; it doesn't erase the substantive critique but complicates reading Chung's motives as purely principled.

## The firings — what actually happened

**First firing (May 2021):** Chung went without authorization to a Red Cross center serving families of a mass shooting. Rosen had him escorted out and fired. An arbitrator found the punishment disproportionate and reduced it to a 30-day suspension. [(Palo Alto Daily Post)](https://padailypost.com/2026/05/13/personal-feud-colors-race-for-da/)

**Second firing (April 2023):** Rosen terminated Chung for attending a training seminar in San Diego in defiance of explicit instructions not to go. A personnel board reversed this to a two-week suspension and reinstated him in June 2023. [(Palo Alto Daily Post)](https://padailypost.com/2026/05/13/personal-feud-colors-race-for-da/) [(Palo Alto Daily Post, Dec 2022)](https://padailypost.com/2022/12/25/rosen-forced-to-re-hire-deputy-da-who-criticized-his-office/)

Chung currently has **three active lawsuits against Rosen**: two related to the firings (First Amendment retaliation; failure to implement the first arbitration award), and a third added in 2025. [(Palo Alto Daily Post, Jul 2025)](https://padailypost.com/2025/07/30/prosecutor-sues-da-rosen-a-third-time-over-job/)

## Stated positions

Source: campaign website [(chungforchange.com/issues)](https://www.chungforchange.com/issues), Davis Vanguard interview [(May 2026)](https://davisvanguard.org/2026/05/prosecutorial-misconduct-police-accountability/).

**Violent and repeat offenders:**
- Eliminate the "revolving door" for repeat and violent offenders
- Create vertical prosecution teams for gun crimes, domestic violence, child abuse, sexual assault
- Supported Prop 36 (targeting repeat offenders); opposed the Prop 47 $950 theft threshold as enabling repeat shoplifting [(chungforchange.com)](https://www.chungforchange.com/issues)

**Victim rights and case speed:**
- Criticizes 5–7 year victim case delays in the current office
- Enforce speedy pleas, hearings, trials, sentencings, restitution; end repeated case continuances [(chungforchange.com)](https://www.chungforchange.com/issues)

**Mental health and addiction:**
- Establish interdisciplinary prosecution teams with embedded mental health and substance abuse experts
- Divert most misdemeanors to conserve resources for serious felonies [(chungforchange.com)](https://www.chungforchange.com/issues)

**Transparency and Brady compliance:**
- Systemic Brady violations in the current office: prosecutors receive only selective information from supervisors about officer credibility before testimony, not direct access to underlying records [(Davis Vanguard)](https://davisvanguard.org/2026/05/prosecutorial-misconduct-police-accountability/)
- Proposal: grant prosecutors, public defenders, and the public greater access to officer credibility findings and disciplinary records
- Partner conviction integrity unit with universities, nonprofits, and law students for wrongful-conviction review [(Davis Vanguard)](https://davisvanguard.org/2026/05/prosecutorial-misconduct-police-accountability/)

**Corrupt public officials:**
- Prosecute law-breaking officials equally — politicians, judges, attorneys, police [(chungforchange.com)](https://www.chungforchange.com/issues)

**Stanford protesters:**
- Called on Rosen to reduce felony charges against pro-Palestine Stanford protesters to misdemeanors before trial; the office was subsequently removed from the retrial by a judge following allegations of improper fundraising by Rosen connected to the pending case [(Bolts Magazine)](https://boltsmag.org/santa-clara-da-jeff-rosen-recused-stanford-student-palestine-protesters-retrial/) [(Davis Vanguard)](https://davisvanguard.org/2026/05/prosecutorial-misconduct-police-accountability/)

**Young defendants:**
- Factor modern brain-development research into charging decisions for offenders under 25 [(Davis Vanguard)](https://davisvanguard.org/2026/05/prosecutorial-misconduct-police-accountability/)

## Controversies / liabilities

- **Fired twice, reinstated twice:** Both firings were overturned by independent arbitrators/personnel boards, which validates that the firings were disproportionate — but the underlying conduct (unauthorized presence at crime scene, defying direct orders on conference attendance) is its own signal about professional discipline.
- **Three active lawsuits against current employer:** Unusual for a job applicant; creates governance questions about his relationship with office staff if he wins.
- **Paid to stay home:** The optics of collecting a paycheck while campaigning full-time against the payer are used by Rosen's camp to frame Chung as an opportunist. Factually accurate; meaning disputed.
- **Self-funded campaign at 3.4:1 financial disadvantage:** Limits reach; unlikely to overcome without major institutional support.
- **2022 loss by 2.3:1:** Lost to Rosen 79K to 182K in a three-way race — a significant defeat in the same county.

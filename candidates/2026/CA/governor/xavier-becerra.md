# Xavier Becerra — 2026 CA Governor candidate

## Background

Becerra, 68, is a lifelong California Democrat who represented a Los Angeles congressional district for 24 years (1993–2017). Governor Jerry Brown appointed him California's Attorney General in January 2017 — the first Latino to hold that office. [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavier_Becerra) After four years as AG, President Biden nominated him as U.S. Secretary of Health and Human Services (2021–2025), where he oversaw COVID vaccine distribution, drug-price negotiations under the Inflation Reduction Act, and major restructuring of HHS sub-agencies. [(Congress.gov bio)](https://www.congress.gov/117/meeting/house/112595/witnesses/HHRG-117-IF14-Bio-BecerraX-20210512.pdf) He announced his gubernatorial candidacy April 2, 2025. As of May 2026, he leads the field with roughly 19% in polling, having surged after Eric Swalwell collapsed. [(CalMatters voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/governor/)

## Record / past performance

**As HHS Secretary (2021–2025):**
- Negotiated Medicare drug pricing for 10 high-cost drugs under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), achieving 38%–79% discounts projected to save Medicare $6 billion annually; a second round of 15 drugs including Ozempic and Ibrance was announced January 17, 2025 (three days before Becerra left office). [(CMS — Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program, primary)](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/negotiating-lower-drug-prices-works-saves-billions) [(CMS — 15 additional drugs announcement, primary)](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/hhs-announces-15-additional-drugs-selected-medicare-drug-price-negotiations-continued-effort-lower)
- Oversaw coverage expansion reaching approximately 14 million additional people, and $35 insulin cap under IRA.
- Oversaw COVID-19 vaccine distribution. Criticized for low public visibility during acute pandemic phases and for confusing federal public-health messaging that eroded public trust.
- Led major restructuring of FDA and CDC sub-agencies.

**As California AG (2017–2021):**
- Filed 122 lawsuits against the first Trump administration (per his campaign biography; KQED independently reported 120 at time of his 2025 gubernatorial announcement). [(xavierbecerra2026.com/bio, primary)](https://www.xavierbecerra2026.com/bio/) [(KQED, secondary)](https://www.kqed.org/news/12034105/xavier-becerra-enters-california-governors-race-citing-break-glass-moment)
- As AG, faced criticism from environmental advocates for perceived reluctance to aggressively litigate against oil companies. [(KQED, secondary, nonpartisan)](https://www.kqed.org/news/12083992/xavier-becerra-says-he-will-fight-for-california-who-did-he-fight-for-as-ag)
- Critics on the progressive side characterized his AG record as light on corporate accountability and policing reform. [(KQED)](https://www.kqed.org/news/12084594/what-ag-xavier-becerra-reveals-about-a-possible-gov-becerra)

**In Congress (1993–2017):**
- 24-year representative from Los Angeles; chaired the House Democratic Caucus. Standard liberal voting record; no anomalous votes surfaced in research.

## Controversies / liabilities

**1. Migrant children / HHS oversight failure (most serious):**
During Becerra's HHS tenure, roughly 85,000 unaccompanied migrant children could not be reached by phone after release to sponsors (per a New York Times analysis and HHS data covering 2021–2023). The HHS Inspector General found documentation gaps in sponsor screening in 16% of sampled case files, and internal HHS officials raised concerns that rushed releases increased child labor trafficking risk. Becerra's public response — claiming children were "not lost, just not picking up the phone" — drew immediate fact-checking contradictions as receipts emerged. [(NBC News, secondary)](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/xavier-becerra-pushback-biden-era-colleagues-california-governor-race-rcna344720) Note: right-leaning outlets (Fox News, 100PercentFedUp, ConservativePlaylist) amplify this issue heavily; left-leaning sources acknowledge real oversight failures while contesting framing of "300,000 lost children."

**2. Campaign aide fraud:**
In November 2025, Becerra's former political consultant (20-year chief of staff) pleaded guilty to federal charges of siphoning approximately $225,000 from his campaign accounts over two years, along with Newsom's former chief of staff. The guilty plea came on the day of a major gubernatorial debate. [(CalMatters debate coverage, secondary)](https://calmatters.org/newsletter/xavier-becerra-last-governor-debate/)

**3. Oil money vs. climate record tension:**
Having accepted Chevron and CRC money via independent expenditures while running on a clean energy platform is being attacked by environmental groups and Steyer. CA Enviro Voters ran explicit opposition. [(EnviroVoters.org, partisan — environmental left)](https://envirovoters.org/becerra-is-wrong-about-big-oil/)

**4. Former Biden colleagues criticism:**
Some former Biden-era DOJ and HHS officials have publicly raised questions about his fitness and management style. The nature is characterized as concerns about accountability and management effectiveness rather than specific misconduct. [(NBC News, secondary)](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/xavier-becerra-pushback-biden-era-colleagues-california-governor-race-rcna344720)

**5. "Status quo" framing:**
Opponents across the debate stage (San Jose Mayor Matt Mahan, Katie Porter, Tom Steyer) attacked him as the embodiment of the Democratic establishment's failures — budget deficits, homelessness, housing costs — during his long career in California and federal politics.

# Matt Mahan — 2026 CA Governor candidate

## Background

Matthew William Mahan, born November 18, 1982, in San Francisco, grew up working-class in Watsonville, CA — his father a union letter carrier, his mother a schoolteacher ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Mahan)). He attended Bellarmine College Preparatory on a low-income scholarship, then graduated *magna cum laude* from Harvard in 2005 (social studies, Harvard Undergraduate Council president) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Mahan)).

After Harvard: two years teaching 7th–8th grade English/history at Alum Rock Middle School via Teach for America (2006–2008) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Mahan)). He then joined **Causes**, a civic-tech startup (a Facebook app connecting nonprofits with supporters) co-founded by Facebook/Napster investor Sean Parker, rising from nonprofit-partnerships director through VP of business development and COO to CEO and president ([Joint Venture profile](https://jointventure.org/read-more-profiles/1724-meet-matt-mahan-brigade); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/07/who-is-matt-mahan-silicon-valley-california-governor-san-jose-mayor/)).

In 2014, Mahan co-founded **Brigade**, a nonpartisan social platform for civic engagement and voter participation — a "Facebook or LinkedIn of politics" — and served as its CEO; it launched with more than $9 million in investment from Sean Parker, venture capitalist Ron Conway, and Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff ([Joint Venture profile](https://jointventure.org/read-more-profiles/1724-meet-matt-mahan-brigade); [SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/01/29/matt-mahan-governor-san-jose-mayor-gavin-newsom/)). Brigade wound down in 2019: its engineering team was acqui-hired by Pinterest while its technology and data (including the Causes assets) were sold off ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/01/29/matt-mahan-governor-san-jose-mayor-gavin-newsom/)). The Brigade period is load-bearing for understanding his frame: he thinks of politics through a civic-product lens — accountability metrics, reducing friction for participation, data-driven outcomes.

He is 43, married to Silvia-Wedad Scandar (met at Harvard), two children, lives in San Jose's Almaden Valley ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Mahan)).

**Political timeline:**
- 2021: Took office on San Jose City Council, District 10 — won the open seat outright in the March 2020 primary with nearly 58% of the vote, succeeding term-limited Johnny Khamis ([San Jose Inside](https://www.sanjoseinside.com/the-fly/matt-mahan-pulls-off-decisive-victory-in-san-joses-d10/))
- November 2022: Elected Mayor of San Jose, defeating labor-backed supervisor Cindy Chavez ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/07/who-is-matt-mahan-silicon-valley-california-governor-san-jose-mayor/))
- January 1, 2023: Became 67th Mayor of San Jose
- November 2024: Re-elected with 86.6% of the vote (144,701 to Tyrone Wade's 22,363 in the final official count) ([San Jose Inside — final official vote count](https://www.sanjoseinside.com/politics/sj-mayor-matt-mahan-wins-easily-as-trio-battles-in-16th-congressional-district/))
- January 29, 2026: Announced candidacy for California governor ([CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/01/governors-race-matt-mahan/))

## Stated positions

Mahan runs on a "back to basics," moderate-Democrat platform. Core message: deliver results without raising taxes or "throwing money at problems" — explicitly positioning against both MAGA and progressive tax-and-spend Democrats ([mahanforcalifornia.com](https://www.mahanforcalifornia.com/)).

**Housing / homelessness:**
- Pause taxes on new homes for two years to spur construction and lower housing costs ([NBC LA](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/matt-mahan-what-to-know-about-california-governor-candidate/3884556/))
- Expand tiny homes and interim housing; mandate treatment for addiction and mental health ([NBC LA](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/matt-mahan-what-to-know-about-california-governor-candidate/3884556/))
- Reduce regulatory burden and streamline permitting: "We've just made it too hard and too expensive to build" ([LAist transcript](https://laist.com/news/politics/2026-election-california-primary-matt-mahan-california-governor-transcript))
- Redirected Measure E funds from permanent affordable housing toward quick-build temporary shelter — a practical approach criticized by affordable-housing advocates as short-termist ([San José Spotlight](https://sanjosespotlight.com/san-jose-mayor-wants-to-permanently-shift-measure-e-affordable-housing-funds/))

**Fiscal:**
- Opposes new taxes, including the state billionaires' tax and gas tax increases ([NBC LA](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/matt-mahan-what-to-know-about-california-governor-candidate/3884556/))
- Proposes suspending the gas tax ([CalMatters voter guide](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/governor/))
- Tie elected officials' pay raises to measurable performance metrics (unemployment, homelessness) ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/07/who-is-matt-mahan-silicon-valley-california-governor-san-jose-mayor/))
- Advocates tax credits and tax increment financing over large bond measures ([LAist transcript](https://laist.com/news/politics/2026-election-california-primary-matt-mahan-california-governor-transcript))

**Public safety / criminal justice:**
- Supported **Proposition 36** (2024) — increased penalties for drug and theft crimes — bucking Newsom and progressive party leadership ([NBC LA](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/matt-mahan-what-to-know-about-california-governor-candidate/3884556/))
- Claims San Jose is "the safest big city in America" under his tenure via community policing + data-driven enforcement ([mahanforcalifornia.com](https://www.mahanforcalifornia.com/))
- Supports technology-based enforcement tools: license plate readers, speed cameras ([NBC LA](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/matt-mahan-what-to-know-about-california-governor-candidate/3884556/))

**Tech / AI:**
- Supports taxing AI companies to fund workforce development ([CalMatters voter guide](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/governor/))
- Backs banning cellphones in K-12 schools; requires parental consent for under-16 social media use ([CalMatters voter guide](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/governor/))
- Requires data-center builders to fully cover their energy costs ([CalMatters voter guide](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/governor/))
- "A human healthcare professional needs to be in the loop" on AI in medicine ([LAist transcript](https://laist.com/news/politics/2026-election-california-primary-matt-mahan-california-governor-transcript))

**Immigration:**
- Opposes local police cooperation with federal immigration enforcement; supports the No Secret Police Act ([NBC LA](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/matt-mahan-what-to-know-about-california-governor-candidate/3884556/))
- Pledges state budget funding to assist immigrant families with essentials (rent, food) during federal enforcement actions ([NBC LA](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/matt-mahan-what-to-know-about-california-governor-candidate/3884556/))

**Education:**
Not a featured campaign pillar. Mahan references his Teach for America background and his wife's work with first-generation low-income students, but detailed K-12 or higher-ed platform positions are not prominently documented as of this writing ([LAist transcript](https://laist.com/news/politics/2026-election-california-primary-matt-mahan-california-governor-transcript)).

## Record / past performance

**Homelessness (San Jose mayor):**
Mahan's signature issue. He proposed — and fought for — a "responsibility to shelter" policy: unhoused individuals who reject three shelter offers within 18 months become eligible for arrest for trespassing ([CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/01/governors-race-matt-mahan/)). His campaign claims a **10% reduction in unsheltered population (2023–2025)** and approximately 1,000 new shelter beds added ([CalMatters voter guide](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/governor/)). He redirected Measure E affordable housing funds toward temporary/tiny-home solutions — faster visible impact but criticized by housing advocates as deprioritizing permanent affordable units ([San José Spotlight](https://sanjosespotlight.com/san-jose-mayor-wants-to-permanently-shift-measure-e-affordable-housing-funds/)).

**Public safety:**
Hired more officers; implemented community policing. Claims San Jose achieved "safest big city" status — a claim that circulates widely in his materials but the specific ranking methodology is not independently verified in sources reviewed here (*inference, not confirmed*).

**Fiscal management:**
Opposed a municipal union wage deal in August 2023, casting the lone "no" vote on contracts giving two large unions a 14.5% raise over three years, citing unfunded pension liabilities and fiscal risk ([San José Spotlight](https://sanjosespotlight.com/san-jose-mayor-matt-mahan-criticizes-union-worker-pay-raises-amid-parks-program-cuts/)). Pledged to end automatic politician pay raises unless performance metrics are met ([mahanforcalifornia.com](https://www.mahanforcalifornia.com/)). Proposed an accountability dashboard for city metrics.

**Government accountability:**
Called for councilmember Omar Torres's resignation after child sexual misconduct allegations surfaced (October 2024) ([San José Spotlight](https://sanjosespotlight.com/breaking-san-jose-officials-call-on-omar-torres-to-resign-amid-child-sex-allegations/)).

**City council pre-mayor:**
Elected 2021, District 10. Limited public record on specific votes available in sources reviewed.

**Brigade (pre-politics):**
Co-founder and CEO of a civic-tech startup aimed at increasing voter participation — ideologically consistent with "good government" moderate positioning ([Joint Venture profile](https://jointventure.org/read-more-profiles/1724-meet-matt-mahan-brigade)). Wound down in 2019; the product didn't achieve mass scale, which is a fair data point on the limits of techno-optimist fixes to civic problems (*inference*).

**Attendance while campaigning:**
Between January–May 2026, Mahan missed more than one-third of San Jose city council meetings (7 of 19) while campaigning, causing quorum delays during budget deficit negotiations — while collecting his full $228,005 salary ([San José Spotlight](https://sanjosespotlight.com/san-jose-mayors-bid-for-ca-governor-fizzles-out/)).

## Controversies / liabilities

**"Billionaire puppet" criticism:** The most consistent attack line from labor and progressive Democrats. His fundraising is overwhelmingly from billionaire tech figures (Brin, Lonsdale, Moritz). Critics question whether he would regulate tech if elected ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/07/who-is-matt-mahan-silicon-valley-california-governor-san-jose-mayor/)). He supports taxing AI companies for workforce development, which could be read as deflection or genuine position — sources do not resolve this clearly.

**FPPC ethics complaint (April 2026):** Raul Claros (founder of nonprofit California Rising) filed a complaint with the California Fair Political Practices Commission alleging illegal coordination between Mahan's campaign and the "California Back to Basics" IE committee — including shared pollsters and a Zoom call where billionaire donors Michael Moritz and Rick Caruso participated in strategy discussions; potential penalties up to $5 million ([Mission Local](https://missionlocal.org/2026/04/matt-mahan-fppc-complaint-moritz-caruso/)). Status as of this writing: filed, not resolved.

**Reed Hastings IE debacle (May 2026):** A pro-Mahan IE committee shut down; another refunded Hastings's $1M donation. Circumstances not fully explained in sources reviewed.

**Council absence while drawing salary:** Missed 33%+ of city council meetings (7 of 19) during campaign, causing quorum problems and delaying budget negotiations while collecting full pay ([San José Spotlight](https://sanjosespotlight.com/san-jose-mayors-bid-for-ca-governor-fizzles-out/)).

**San José Spotlight editorial (January 2026):** Local publication called on him not to run, citing a pattern of never finishing a term in his elected roles and failing to deliver promised changes on homelessness, housing, and public safety ([San José Spotlight](https://sanjosespotlight.com/editorial-san-jose-mayor-should-not-run-for-governor/)).

**Prop 36 split:** Supported increasing drug/theft penalties in a year when most state Democratic leaders opposed it. Could be read as pragmatic centrism or as alienating the base he'd need to win a general election.

**Housing fund redirection:** Shifting Measure E money from permanent affordable housing to temporary shelters produced faster optics but may have deferred the underlying supply problem — a genuine policy liability if homelessness worsens statewide.

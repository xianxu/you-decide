# Antonio Villaraigosa — 2026 CA Governor candidate

## Background

Antonio Ramón Villaraigosa (born January 23, 1953, East Los Angeles) is the 41st Mayor of Los Angeles (2005–2013) and a former Speaker of the California State Assembly (1998–2000). He grew up in East LA, was expelled from high school, later earned a GED and a degree from UCLA. He led East LA student walkouts in 1968 protesting educational inequities — a formative political origin.

He ran for governor in 2018 and placed third in the primary with 13.3% (behind Newsom 33.7%, Cox 25.4%). He announced his 2026 bid on July 23, 2024. Since leaving the mayor's office he worked in the private sector and as an infrastructure adviser to Governor Newsom (2023–2024) — see Controversies section. [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Villaraigosa) [(Ballotpedia)](https://ballotpedia.org/Antonio_Villaraigosa)

All positions are from the candidate's own campaign site unless noted.

    **Housing/affordability**: Proposes $25 billion in revenue bonds for second mortgages for middle-income first-time buyers of homes priced $600k–$1M. Supports fast-tracking home construction and streamlining CEQA permitting. Wants to restore the path to homeownership via expanded CalHFA lending. **Energy/climate**: "All of the Above" energy policy — endorses renewables (solar, wind) alongside nuclear, natural gas, and gas-powered cars. Wants a moratorium on climate regulations. Calls California's aggressive climate timeline unrealistic. Prioritizes building transmission infrastructure (currently takes over a decade). Frames oil and gas as a "transition fuel." [(antonio2026.com/issues)](https://www.antonio2026.com/issues) [(CalMatters voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/governor/)

**Public safety**: Advocates fully funding police departments while investing in community-based prevention. As mayor, he pledged and delivered 1,000 new LAPD officers — LAPD reached its largest force in city history in 2009. Endorsed by PORAC (Peace Officers Research Association). [(antonio2026.com/issues)](https://www.antonio2026.com/issues)

**Education**: Calls education "the civil rights issue of our time." Pro-charter school expansion; fought teachers unions as mayor over seniority-based layoffs and teacher evaluations. Supports evidence-based investment over broad spending. [(LUSD/UCLA piece)](https://luskin.ucla.edu/villaraigosa-education-is-the-civil-rights-issue-of-our-time)

**Immigration/ICE**: On day one would sign emergency executive orders "holding ICE accountable." California would cooperate with ICE only if agents complete 40 hours of California peace-officer training on use of force and search-and-seizure. [(antonio2026.com/issues)](https://www.antonio2026.com/issues)

**Infrastructure**: Wants to expand energy grids, water storage, and transportation. Proposes reforming CEQA to cut permitting delays. Supports the Purple Line extension legacy from his mayoral tenure. [(antonio2026.com/issues)](https://www.antonio2026.com/issues)

**Federal/Trump**: Commits to opposing federal funding cuts and "standing against" Trump administration policies. [(antonio2026.com/issues)](https://www.antonio2026.com/issues)

**Cryptocurrency**: Supports consumer right to use crypto; wants regulatory clarity. (No strong progressive opposition to crypto industry.) [(antonio2026.com/issues)](https://www.antonio2026.com/issues)

**Positioning note**: Villaraigosa is the most centrist/moderate of the major Democratic candidates. He is skeptical of California's progressive regulatory agenda, particularly on climate and CEQA. He attracts labor (trades, construction, police) but not teachers unions. [(CalMatters)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/governor/)

## Record / past performance

**LA Mayor (2005–2013):**

- *Transit*: Championed "Subway to the Sea" (Purple Line to Santa Monica). Led successful campaign for Measure R in 2008 — a half-cent sales tax that generated ~$40 billion over 30 years for transit. Significant infrastructure legacy. [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Villaraigosa)
- *Public safety*: Grew LAPD to its largest force in history by 2009. Crime fell during his tenure (consistent with national trend). [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Villaraigosa)
- *Education reform*: Attempted to seize mayoral control of LAUSD — struck down as unconstitutional in 2006. Created Mayor's Partnership for Los Angeles Schools managing 22 underperforming campuses with measurable gains. Pushed LAUSD to open 36 schools to charter operators. Fought UTLA (teachers union) in court to limit seniority layoff protections and link evaluations to test scores. Called union the "loudest opponent and largest obstacle to creating quality schools." [(ERIC/Education Next)](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ910558) [(The 74)](https://www.the74million.org/article/antonio-villaraigosa-talks-la-charter-expansion-the-friedrichs-case-at-the-supreme-court-and-a-possible-run-for-governor/)
- *Fiscal*: Left office with a 47% approval rating. Critics note he spent significant time on national travel/fundraising rather than direct city governance. Presided through the 2008 recession; LA's fiscal position was strained but not uniquely mismanaged. [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Villaraigosa)

## Controversies / liabilities

1. **Extramarital affair (2007)**: His marriage to Corina Raigosa ended after his affair with television reporter Mirthala Salinas became public during his mayoral tenure. He remarried (Patricia Govea, 2016) and separated again in 2018. He was publicly Catholic and family-values-adjacent while conducting the affair. [(NBCNews — Telemundo suspension)](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna20100417)

2. **Ethics violations (2007, 2010)**: LA Ethics Commission investigated him for 31 campaign finance violations related to his 2003 city council campaign. Separately, he was fined a record $41,849 combined by the FPPC and LA Ethics Commission for failing to report 81 event tickets (Lakers games, Oscars, American Idol finals, etc.). [(LAist — ticket probe)](https://laist.com/news/villaraigosa-ticket-gate-district-a) [(NBC Los Angeles / AP — fine)](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/politics/mayor-antonio-villaraigosa-fine/1920348/) [(FPPC stipulation — Tier A)](https://www.fppc.ca.gov/content/dam/fppc/documents/Stipulations/2011/April/30-Villarigosa.pdf)

3. **Herbalife advisory role**: He worked as a senior adviser to Herbalife's Chairman and CEO, a controversial multi-level marketing company. LULAC head Brent Wilkes expressed surprise and disappointment, citing Herbalife's misleading claims to the roughly 60% Latino distributor base; LULAC, the Hispanic Federation, and the National Consumer's League had petitioned the FTC to investigate the company. Raises questions about post-office influence monetization. [(LAist — LULAC reaction)](https://laist.com/news/kpcc-archive/villaraigosa-s-new-gig-with-herbalife-surprises-di)

4. **Newsom $380k infrastructure job**: Governor Newsom arranged for Villaraigosa to be paid ~$380k over ~10 months to advise on infrastructure, funneled through California Forward (a nonprofit). When costs doubled, California Forward solicited corporate donors — not publicly disclosed until CalMatters/LAist reporting in 2025. California Common Cause called California Forward a "clearinghouse" allowing special interests to pay for the work without standard disclosure. This arrangement is a quid-pro-quo concern: Newsom paid a political rival via opaque nonprofit money from undisclosed corporate interests. [(CalMatters)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2025/06/california-newsom-villaraigosa-infrastructure-project/) [(LAist)](https://laist.com/news/politics/newsom-gave-his-political-rival-a-380k-job-see-the-special-interests-who-paid-for-it)

5. **2012 DNC voice vote incident**: As convention chair, he declared a platform amendment passed on the voice vote despite audible "nays" being louder than "ayes" on three consecutive attempts. Widely seen as a procedurally dishonest moment on camera. [(NPR — DNC platform boos)](https://www.npr.org/2012/09/06/160686513/revised-platform-elicits-boos-at-dnc-in-charlotte) [(Roll Call — DNC platform amendment)](https://rollcall.com/2012/09/05/dnc-amends-platform-amid-controversy/)

6. **Teachers union hostility**: His confrontational relationship with UTLA (called them the "most powerful defenders of the unacceptable status quo") earned him enduring opposition from the teachers union, which is a major Democratic primary force. This is a liability in the primary, though potentially a credential to moderates. [(Dominique DiPrima)](https://www.dominiquediprima.com/villaraigosa-slams-teachers-union-as-roadblock-to-reform-is-he-right/)

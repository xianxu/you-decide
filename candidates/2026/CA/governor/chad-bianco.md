# Chad Bianco — 2026 CA Governor candidate

## Background

Chad Bianco was born October 9, 1967, at Hill Air Force Base, Utah. He moved to California in 1989 and graduated top of his class from the San Bernardino Sheriff's Academy in 1993, joining the Riverside County Sheriff's Department shortly after. He was elected Riverside County Sheriff in 2018 and re-elected in 2022 with ~60% of the vote. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Bianco) — bio only)

He announced his candidacy for governor on February 17, 2025, as the first major Republican to enter the race. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Bianco) — bio only) He lives in Woodcrest, CA with his wife Denise and four adult children; attends Sandals Church in Riverside. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Bianco) — bio only)

Polling as of May 2026: low teens (approximately 16% in UC Berkeley poll, trailing Steve Hilton at 17%). ([NBC Los Angeles](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/chad-bianco-trump-steve-hilton-california-governor-race/3872118/))

## Record / past performance

**COVID enforcement refusal:** Bianco publicly refused to enforce COVID-19 public health mandates and refused vaccination for himself. He tested positive for COVID-19 in January 2021 with mild symptoms. In a December 2020 YouTube video that gained national attention, he accused Governor Newsom of "strongarming" counties into enforcing stay-at-home orders, and stated he would not "make criminals out of business owners, single moms and otherwise healthy individuals." ([Police1 — video coverage](https://www.police1.com/coronavirus-covid-19/articles/video-of-calif-sheriff-chad-biancos-refusal-to-enforce-covid-orders-gets-national-attention-2hmkNIYo1AbIkuGM/)) His refusal also extended to his own deputies — he stated he would not enforce vaccine mandates on department employees. ([NBC San Diego](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/local/politically-speaking-chad-bianco-thinks-california-needs-fixing/4017986/); [Patch/Temecula — vaccine mandate refusal](https://patch.com/california/temecula/riverside-county-sheriff-doubles-down-against-vaccine-mandates)) He has since cited this as a point of pride in his campaign.

**Jail deaths and CA DOJ investigation:** In 2022, Riverside County jails recorded 18 inmate deaths, the highest since 2005 (vs. a historical average of ~7/year). Bianco stated "every single one of these inmate deaths was out of anyone's control." In February 2023, California Attorney General Rob Bonta launched a California Department of Justice investigation into alleged "unconstitutional policing" and problematic jail conditions. ([Prison Legal News, Aug 2024](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/2024/aug/15/after-spike-jail-deaths-riverside-county-sheriffs-department-under-california-department-justice-investigation/) — citing CA DOJ and county records; [ABC7 — civil lawsuits](https://abc7.com/riverside-county-jail-deaths-inmates-sheriff-chad-bianco-in-custody-death/13330033/)) Note: an NBC News report alleged a *federal* DOJ investigation as well, but this could not be independently confirmed by Black Voice News or other outlets; the confirmed investigation is the CA DOJ probe.

**Crime clearance rate:** During Bianco's first six years (2019–2024), Riverside sheriff's deputies cleared an average of just 9.2% of reported Part 1 crimes — less than half the state average and last among California's 57 sheriffs with arrest authority. Property crime clearance was 4.4% (vs. 10.1% for neighboring San Bernardino Sheriff). ([Center on Juvenile and Criminal Justice, 2025](https://www.cjcj.org/reports-publications/fact-sheet/the-sheriff-with-californias-worst-crime-solving-record-is-running-for-governor) — sourced from CA DOJ Open Justice, Crimes and Clearances dataset, 2025)

**Immigration cooperation:** In February 2025, Bianco supported proposed legislation to weaken California's sanctuary state law limiting ICE cooperation. ([CalMatters, Feb 2025](https://calmatters.org/justice/2025/02/sanctuary-state-amador-sheriff/)) His practical stance on ICE cooperation has been inconsistent — see Stated positions above.

**Trump rally arrest (October 2024):** Bianco ordered the arrest of Vem Miller near a Trump rally in Coachella on October 12, 2024, claiming it "probably" thwarted a "third assassination attempt." The FBI and Secret Service said they did not believe Miller was a threat. Miller filed a $100 million federal defamation lawsuit alleging Bianco "fabricated the allegations" to look heroic; the lawsuit was dismissed, and Miller was ordered in January 2026 to complete a gun-safety course and 40 hours of community service. ([ABC7 Los Angeles](https://abc7.com/post/vem-miller-man-arrested-weapons-trump-rally-coachella-sues-riverside-county-sheriff-defamation/15435204/); [Fox11 Los Angeles](https://www.foxla.com/news/vem-miller-lawsuit-riverside-county-chad-bianco))

**BLM moment (2020):** Bianco briefly knelt with Black Lives Matter protesters in June 2020 but later characterized the protests as "destructive riots." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Bianco) — bio only; no Tier-B source located for this specific episode)

## Controversies / liabilities

**Oath Keepers membership (documented, multi-source):**
A September 2021 data breach of Oath Keepers records (published by Distributed Denial of Secrets) exposed Bianco as a former member. He acknowledged joining in February 2014 as a sheriff's lieutenant, paying one year's dues by credit card (~$40), and receiving a U.S. Constitution booklet — and stated he did not renew. He initially said he "don't even remember joining." The 2021 original reporting was by LAist's Frank Stoltze; NPR covered the story the same week. During the May 5, 2026 CNN gubernatorial debate, when pressed, he stated "I'm a proud oath keeper" — framing it as pride in the oaths he took during his law enforcement career, while denying he considered himself a member of the organization. Candidates and moderators challenged his framing. Several Oath Keepers members were convicted for their roles in the January 6, 2021 Capitol attack.

**Ballot seizure (April 2026):**
Bianco ordered the seizure of approximately 650,000 ballots from California's 2025 election, framed as an election integrity investigation. The California Supreme Court halted the investigation. When CalMatters obtained the unsealed warrants, the evidentiary basis was found to rest "solely on claims from a citizens' activist group" — characterized as the equivalent of "flat earthers" — with no independent forensic analysis and no direct evidence of voter fraud. Riverside County's Board of Supervisors voted 4-1 to decline paying outside counsel to defend Bianco in four resulting lawsuits.
- ([CalMatters investigation](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/04/chad-bianco-election-warrants/))

# Tom Steyer — 2026 CA Governor candidate

## Background

Thomas Fahr Steyer (born June 27, 1957, Manhattan) is a billionaire hedge fund founder turned climate and political activist. He is a graduate of Phillips Exeter Academy (valedictorian), Yale (economics/political science), and Stanford (MBA) [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Steyer).

He founded Farallon Capital Management in 1986 with $15 million in seed capital, growing it to a $20 billion hedge fund before departing in 2012. He signed the Giving Pledge (commitment to donate most wealth during lifetime) and has described his 2012 pivot as "walking away from business to pursue public service" [(tomsteyer.com)](https://www.tomsteyer.com/).

Forbes estimated his net worth at $2.4 billion as of April 2026 [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Steyer).

He announced his 2026 California governor candidacy in November 2025 to succeed term-limited Gavin Newsom.

## Stated positions

All positions sourced from the candidate's official campaign site [(tomsteyer.com/issues)](https://www.tomsteyer.com/issues) unless otherwise noted.

**Housing:** "Build One Million Homes You Can Afford" — launching what the campaign calls the largest state-level affordable housing effort in California history.

**Taxation:** "Make Corporations and Billionaires (Like Me) Pay More Taxes" — closing corporate tax loopholes, shutting the "water's edge" loophole for multinationals, and floating a 2027 special election to increase commercial property taxes (split-roll). Steyer has **not** endorsed the union-backed one-time billionaire wealth-tax ballot initiative; he focuses on corporate structural reform rather than that proposal. (Thurmond is the only top-tier candidate supporting the wealth-tax initiative — see thurmond profile.) [(KQED)](https://www.kqed.org/news/12079441/heres-how-californias-next-governor-will-change-your-taxes)

**Utilities:** Break up utility monopolies to lower electric bills by 25%.

**Education:** Free education from early childhood through college; fund it by making corporations pay more; goal of making California a top-ten state for public education.

**Immigration:** Abolish ICE; California to criminally prosecute ICE agents and leadership; expand immigration legal-defense infrastructure; end racial profiling via state legislation [(tomsteyer.com/issues/abolish-ice)](https://www.tomsteyer.com/issues/abolish-ice); full plan announced April 2026 [(tomsteyer.com press)](https://www.tomsteyer.com/press/in-new-ad-steyer-calls-to-abolish-ice-and-prosecute-agents).

**Healthcare:** As of December 2025, reversed his prior opposition to single-payer, stating he had been "wrong" and that "Bernie Sanders was right" [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Steyer). Now supports universal healthcare access for all Californians.

**Climate/Environment:** Defending California's climate and environmental protections; central to his identity as founder of NextGen America.

**Campaign finance:** Ban corporate PAC money from California elections.

**AI:** Make AI advances benefit all Californians equitably (platform page; no specifics documented).

**Film & Entertainment, Caregiving, Gas/Oil prices:** Listed on campaign site as policy areas; detailed policy text not documented in reviewed sources.

## Record / past performance

**Farallon Capital (1986–2012):** Built one of the world's largest hedge funds. A July 4, 2014 New York Times investigation (Barbaro and Davenport, "Aims of Donor Are Shadowed by Past in Coal") found that coal mines Farallon invested in under Steyer's management increased annual production by approximately 70 million tons — more than Britain consumes per year [(NYT, July 4 2014)](https://www.nytimes.com/2014/07/05/us/politics/aims-of-donor-are-shadowed-by-past-in-coal.html). SEC filings show Farallon accumulated approximately $90 million in Corrections Corporation of America (now CoreCivic), peaking at roughly 5.5% of outstanding shares in mid-2005, before divesting by early 2006 [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Steyer). Steyer has called both "mistakes" and states the positions were sold; claims of continued exposure through offshore vehicles after divestiture remain disputed and not independently verified in Tier-A/B sources reviewed.

**NextGen America (founded 2013):** Environmental advocacy nonprofit and PAC. By 2014, Steyer had invested nearly $74 million in elections through this vehicle, focused on climate and youth voter mobilization [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Steyer).

**2020 Presidential campaign:** Spent over $253 million, nearly all personal funds, making it one of the most expensive self-funded presidential campaigns in history. Participated in six televised debates. Withdrew after finishing third in South Carolina with 11% of the vote, receiving no pledged delegates [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Steyer).

**California ballot campaigns:** Campaign cites a record of running ballot initiatives against Big Oil on climate, closing corporate tax loopholes, raising cigarette taxes, and securing funds for healthcare and public schools [(tomsteyer.com)](https://www.tomsteyer.com/). These are claims on the candidate's own site; independent verification not reviewed in this research pass.

**Campaign finance / self-funding:**

Steyer is self-funding almost entirely. As of late April 2026, he had contributed approximately $147 million of the campaign's $147.3 million total — essentially 100% personal funds [(calmatters.org)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/04/california-governor-race-financials/). This already exceeds the $73 million Newsom spent on his 2021 recall defense, and positions Steyer's 2026 run as potentially the most expensive gubernatorial campaign in California history.

For comparison, rival Democratic candidates' total fundraising: Matt Mahan $13 million, Katie Porter $2.8 million, Xavier Becerra $1 million, Antonio Villaraigosa $707,000, Tony Thurmond $62,000 [(calmatters.org)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/04/california-governor-race-financials/). Steyer outspent rivals 20- to 30-fold through mid-April.

**Paid influencers:** Steyer's campaign paid over $123,400 to at least eight influencers from January through April 18, 2026, including $100,000 to Texas-based Latino mega-influencer Carlos Eduardo Espina (14.3 million TikTok followers) [(calmatters.org influencer piece)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/05/california-governor-race-influencers/).

## Controversies / liabilities

**Coal and private prison investments (past):** The most prominent contradiction in his record. Despite building a climate-advocacy brand through NextGen America, his Farallon tenure involved funding coal expansion (NYT, July 4 2014) and private prison companies (CoreCivic, which runs ICE detention facilities; SEC filings via Wikipedia). Running in 2026 on an "abolish ICE" platform while having profited from ICE detention infrastructure is a documented line of attack [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Steyer). Steyer acknowledges these as past mistakes; Farallon divested CoreCivic by early 2006 per SEC filings.

**Healthcare flip-flop:** Opposed Medicare for All in his 2020 presidential run; reversed in December 2025, calling himself "wrong." Opponents may use this as evidence of opportunistic position-shifting [(Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Steyer).

**Billionaire spending paradox:** Simultaneously campaigns against billionaire influence in politics while spending $147 million of his own money to win the governorship. The American Prospect notes that the volume of his advertising has convinced many voters he is "one mega-rich guy," undermining his reformist credibility — even among progressive groups he courts [(prospect.org)](https://prospect.org/2026/05/21/tom-steyers-problem-california-governor-race/).

**Polling underperformance relative to spend:** Despite $132–147 million in spending, Steyer polled at 17% in the May 9–10 Emerson survey — tied with Republican Steve Hilton (17%) and trailing Democrat Xavier Becerra (19%); in the prior April Emerson poll he was at 14% [(Emerson, May 2026)](https://emersoncollegepolling.com/california-2026-poll-becerra-continues-to-surge-steyer-and-hilton-compete-for-second-spot/), [(Emerson, April 2026)](https://emersoncollegepolling.com/california-2026-poll-april/) [(prospect.org)](https://prospect.org/2026/05/21/tom-steyers-problem-california-governor-race/). His return-on-spend is poor by any measure.

**No governance experience:** No elected office held. His record is advocacy, ballot campaigns, and a failed presidential bid. No executive or legislative track record to evaluate.

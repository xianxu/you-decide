# Joaquin Jiménez — 2026 SM County Supervisor D3

## Background

Jiménez immigrated to Half Moon Bay from Mexico at age 13 and has lived in San Mateo County for nearly 40 years. BA from CSU Chico (sociology; Chicano studies minor). Career path: teaching → migrant education programs → county juvenile probation. Founder of **Rancho San Benito** (helps farmworkers become independent farmers/business owners). Program Director at **ALAS** (Ayudando Latinos A Soñar), a coastside social-services nonprofit. Farmworker liaison at **Puente de la Costa Sur**, which runs south-coast programs.

In 2020 became the first Mexican immigrant elected to the Half Moon Bay City Council (District 3). Served 2020–2024, including term(s) as mayor — the city's first immigrant mayor. Lost reelection in November 2024 to Paul Nagengast (~59% vs ~41%). Now running for the County Supervisor seat held by incumbent Ray Mueller, whom Jiménez initially endorsed and helped introduce to coastside farmworker communities in 2022.

District 3 covers San Carlos, Menlo Park, the coast (Half Moon Bay, El Granada, Pescadero, Montara, Pacifica).

## Stated Positions

**Housing / farmworker.** Championed 555 Kelly Ave senior farmworker housing development (not yet broken ground as of 2026). Backed Stone Pine Cove (46-unit farmworker housing project). Opposes "one-size-fits-all housing solutions" lacking local infrastructure funding; opposes forcing housing on cities without adequate infrastructure. Supports homeownership programs for low-income residents.

**Agriculture.** Wants county to support coastal farming as economic development — farmworker training, directing locally grown food to food banks and hospitals. Founded Equity Express Farm Bus. Created Rancho San Benito to grow farmworker entrepreneurship.

**Public safety.** Advocates for expanded Sheriff community engagement on the Coastside — presence at events like the Pumpkin Festival so vulnerable populations feel "connected and comfortable" with law enforcement. No defund framing.

**Environment.** Opposes Pacifica Quarry development (to preserve Timigtac, a historic Native village). Water resource protection, greywater/recycled water systems, PFAS contamination concern. Describes himself as a steward of "farmers, ranchers, farmworkers and open spaces."

**Transportation.** Proposes Highway 92 alternative emergency routes; equitable telecom expansion.

**Budget.** Prioritize "financially sustainable programs serving those with greatest need." Opposes what he calls wasteful discretionary spending — cited a $20,000 county grant to the Pacifica Historical Society as example.

**Sheriff Corpus / Measure A.** A defining distinction from Mueller: Jiménez stood by removed Sheriff Christina Corpus "100%" before her removal. Called Measure A (which authorized supervisors to remove the sheriff) "racist" and "sexist." Argues recall processes are more democratic than board removal of an elected official.

# Ray Mueller — 2026 SM County Supervisor D3

## Background

Attorney and disaster-management professional. UC Berkeley (bioresource sciences), UC Hastings School of Law. Career arc: litigation attorney (catastrophic-injury cases) → attorney/director of technology applications at IEM (emergency response/crisis management firm). Early in career, served as Chief of Staff to Santa Clara County Supervisor and State Senator Joe Simitian. Chaired Menlo Park Transportation Commission before joining Menlo Park City Council in 2012, where he served ~10 years including a term as mayor (2019 — described as last city-wide elected council member/mayor under the old at-large system). First elected to San Mateo County Board of Supervisors District 3 in 2022, defeating a crowded field. District 3 spans Menlo Park, Atherton, Portola Valley, Woodside, San Carlos, Belmont (partial), Pacifica, Half Moon Bay, and unincorporated coastside communities.

[Sources: Palo Alto Daily Post, The Almanac 2022 editorial, SM Daily Journal background piece — Tier B]

## Stated Positions

**Housing.** Supported the county's Housing Element Update (adopted March 2025) but opposed a specific April 2026 Housing Element amendment, explicitly stating: "I can't support the amount of density that's with" [it]. Expressed skepticism on the regional housing bond timing (December 2023), citing inadequate community outreach phase rather than opposition to the bond itself ("We are committed to this effort"). Requires city collaboration/buy-in before pursuing county-level affordable housing ballot measures: "I can't see myself doing this without a formal acknowledgement of some sort by the cities that they are supportive." Backed $1M for farmworker housing (Measure K funds, Half Moon Bay Farmworker Project — 45–50 manufactured units). [Sources: SM Daily Journal, smcgov.org, CBS SF — Tier A/B]

**Public safety / Sheriff oversight.** Led the board's effort to remove Sheriff Christina Corpus after documented misconduct. Co-authored statements (with Supervisor Noelia Corzo) calling out possible retaliation when Corpus fired a cooperating deputy. Launched county's first Residential Burglary Reward Program ($10,000 for information leading to arrest). Established urgent care pilot clinic on the coast after the region's only ER closed. [Sources: Redwood City Pulse, smcgov.org official statements, Local News Matters — Tier A/B]

**Budget / tax.** One of two supervisors who declined to support studying a county sales tax increase. Proposed prioritizing Measure K-funded services by most-to-least essential (6-month vs. 18-month funding tiers for least vs. most critical services). [Sources: SM Daily Journal — Tier B]

**Labor / workers.** Pushed for the county Office of Labor Standards and Enforcement (workplace conditions, wage theft protection). [Sources: Local News Matters — Tier B]

**Public health / regulation.** Led bans on recreational nitrous oxide and kratom (first Bay Area county). Established Office of Labor Standards and Enforcement. [Sources: Local News Matters — Tier B]

**Sanctuary / immigration cooperation.** No explicit public statement found on Sheriff-ICE cooperation posture. His leadership of the Corpus removal process (which included Corpus's mixed record on federal cooperation) implies some oversight engagement, but specific sanctuary policy votes not found in available sources. ## Controversies / Liabilities

**Housing density resistance:** His April 2026 no vote on the Housing Element amendment and his stated discomfort with density levels could read as NIMBY-adjacent. The city-collaboration prerequisite for affordable housing ballot measures similarly limits his builder posture. These are not scandals but are policy liabilities on the zoning-realism axis.

**Regional bond procedural objection (2023):** Voted against the board studying a regional housing bond (on timing/process grounds, not substance). Read charitably as procedural; read critically as obstacle-raising.

**No identified ethical or misconduct concerns.** No FPPC filing lapses, no conflict-of-interest issues found in sourced material. Clean personal record.

**Partisan framing risk:** Strong Newsom endorsement positions him squarely in CA Democratic establishment. In a county that skews heavily Democratic, this is an asset, but it may reinforce "tax and spend" association for a fiscally-conservative voter.

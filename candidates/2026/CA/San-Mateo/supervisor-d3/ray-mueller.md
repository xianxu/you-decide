# Ray Mueller — 2026 SM County Supervisor D3

## Background

Attorney and disaster-management professional. UC Berkeley (bioresource sciences), UC Hastings School of Law. Career arc: litigation attorney (catastrophic-injury cases) → attorney/director of technology applications at IEM (emergency response/crisis management firm). Early in career, served as Chief of Staff to Santa Clara County Supervisor and State Senator Joe Simitian. Chaired Menlo Park Transportation Commission before joining Menlo Park City Council in 2012, where he served ~10 years including a term as mayor (2019 — described as last city-wide elected council member/mayor under the old at-large system). First elected to San Mateo County Board of Supervisors District 3 in 2022, defeating a crowded field. District 3 spans Menlo Park, Atherton, Portola Valley, Woodside, San Carlos, Belmont (partial), Pacifica, Half Moon Bay, and unincorporated coastside communities.

[Sources: Palo Alto Daily Post, The Almanac 2022 editorial, SM Daily Journal background piece — Tier B]

## Stated Positions

**Housing.** Supported the county's Housing Element Update (adopted March 2025) but voted no on a specific April 2026 Housing Element amendment over density, stating: "I can't support the amount of density that's within this currently at the El Granada sites." He subsequently voted yes on a Bayside-only rezoning ordinance after being assured it did not include the El Granada sites. [[Tier B — Coastside News, BOS 4/21/2026](https://www.coastsidenews.com/coastie/san-mateo-county-board-of-supervisors-4-21-2026---locunity-reports/article_6fc2b562-a8d3-458e-89f3-962b8847e151.html)] Expressed skepticism on the regional housing bond timing (December 2023), citing inadequate community outreach phase rather than opposition to the bond itself ("We are committed to this effort"). Requires city collaboration/buy-in before pursuing county-level affordable housing ballot measures: "I can't see myself doing this without a formal acknowledgement of some sort by the cities that they are supportive." Backed $1M for farmworker housing (Measure K funds, Half Moon Bay Farmworker Project — 45–50 manufactured units). [Sources: SM Daily Journal, smcgov.org, CBS SF — Tier A/B]

**Public safety / Sheriff oversight.** Co-led the board's effort to remove Sheriff Christina Corpus, pressing publicly for the board to act efficiently on the removal process ("Fairness doesn't just apply to the Sheriff, it applies to those who work in the Sheriff's office and the residents of this County"). [[Tier A — smcgov.org](https://www.smcgov.org/ceo/news/statements-supervisors-ray-mueller-and-noelia-corzo-regarding-vote-measure)] Launched county's first Residential Burglary Reward Program ($10,000 for information leading to arrest); established an urgent-care pilot clinic on the coast after the region's only ER closed. ## Controversies / Liabilities

**Housing density resistance:** His April 2026 no vote on the Housing Element amendment and his stated discomfort with density levels could read as NIMBY-adjacent. The city-collaboration prerequisite for affordable housing ballot measures similarly limits his builder posture. These are not scandals but are policy liabilities on the zoning-realism axis.

**Regional bond procedural objection (2023):** Voted against the board studying a regional housing bond (on timing/process grounds, not substance). Read charitably as procedural; read critically as obstacle-raising.

**No identified ethical or misconduct concerns.** No FPPC filing lapses, no conflict-of-interest issues found in sourced material. Clean personal record.

**Partisan framing risk:** Strong Newsom endorsement positions him squarely in CA Democratic establishment. In a county that skews heavily Democratic, this is an asset, but it may reinforce "tax and spend" association for a fiscally-conservative voter.

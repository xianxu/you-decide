# Eleni Kounalakis — 2026 CA Treasurer candidate

## Background

Eleni Kounalakis is a Democrat and California's Lieutenant Governor, serving her second term. Her ballot designation in this race is "Lieutenant Governor of California." Before elected office she spent roughly two decades at her family's firm, AKT Development Corporation, a Sacramento-area real-estate company founded by her father, developer Angelo Tsakopoulos, where her work centered on housing development and infrastructure financing. She served as U.S. Ambassador to Hungary from 2010 to 2013 under President Obama, then won the lieutenant governorship in 2018 and re-election in 2022 — a role that carries ex-officio seats on the UC Board of Regents, the CSU Board of Trustees, and the State Lands Commission. Her voter-guide self-description is "Businesswoman, financial expert." Her 2024 financial disclosures reported personal investment income of at least $1.39 million. [(LAist voter guide)](https://laist.com/news/politics/voter-guides/2026-election-california-primary-california-treasurer) [(CalMatters profile)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2025/04/california-governor-eleni-kounalakis/)

She entered the Treasurer's race as the front-runner on money and name identification: per CalMatters she held roughly nine times the cash on hand of the other five candidates combined, in a race that has drawn more than $10 million in total. [(CalMatters race story)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/05/california-treasurer-election-2026/)

**About the field:** This is an open seat — incumbent Treasurer Fiona Ma is termed out and running for Lieutenant Governor. The June 2, 2026 top-two primary draws a six-candidate field: Eleni Kounalakis (D), Anna Caballero (D), Tony Vazquez (D), Jennifer Hawks (R), David Serpa (R), and Glenn Turner (Green). Reporting widely frames the contest as a likely two-Democrat runoff between Kounalakis and Caballero, with Kounalakis cast as the favorite ("Kounalakis' race to lose"); there is no public head-to-head polling, so viability framing rests on money, name ID, and reporter assessment rather than survey data (inference). [(CalMatters race story)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/05/california-treasurer-election-2026/) [(KPBS)](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/05/05/in-the-six-way-race-to-be-californias-treasurer-it-likely-comes-down-to-two-democrats)

**About the office she seeks:** The California State Treasurer is the state's banker and its debt and investment manager — issuing and managing state bonds, running the roughly $200 billion-plus Pooled Money Investment Account, and administering savings programs including ScholarShare 529, CalABLE, and CalSavers. The Treasurer also sits on the boards of CalPERS and CalSTRS and chairs or serves on dozens of state finance and investment authorities. [(CalMatters voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/treasurer/) [(LAist voter guide)](https://laist.com/news/politics/voter-guides/2026-election-california-primary-california-treasurer)

## Controversies / liabilities

**1. State-agency office leasing + absence of a blind trust (best-corroborated flag in the field):** Kounalakis is a partner in Meridian Plaza, a Sacramento office building that leases space to state agencies — including the Department of Public Health and a Department of General Services branch — alongside roughly 34 other tenants, many of them lobbying firms (trade groups for the auto, insurance, beer-distribution, and oil industries, plus Microsoft, PG&E, Verizon, and Altria). CalMatters reported the arrangement generated more than $340,000 in rental income. As Lieutenant Governor she has not placed her assets in a blind trust, and has pledged to do so only "if elected governor." Ethics experts Tom Hiltachk and John Pelissero flagged the conflict in CalMatters' reporting. [(CalMatters profile)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2025/04/california-governor-eleni-kounalakis/)

A "Newsom-appointment pay-to-play" framing has circulated but is not corroborated by the sourced reporting and is not asserted here; the documented issue is the state-agency leasing combined with the blind-trust gap. The most direct write-up of the leasing arrangement appeared in California Globe, which is a Tier-C partisan outlet — CalMatters is used as the anchor for this claim.

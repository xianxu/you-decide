# Richard Barrera — 2026 CA Superintendent of Public Instruction candidate

## Background

**About the office.** The Superintendent of Public Instruction (SPI) is California's nonpartisan elected head of K-12 education and runs the California Department of Education (CDE), overseeing schooling for nearly 6 million students against an ~$150 billion budget. The office has limited direct power: the Governor-appointed State Board of Education sets much policy, the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) leaves most budget and curriculum decisions to local districts, and the Governor controls the overall budget. The SPI's real levers are curriculum frameworks, instructional-materials approval, statewide literacy/assessment initiatives, and the bully pulpit. The 2026 seat is open because incumbent Tony Thurmond ran for Governor; the June 2, 2026 primary is top-two (the two highest finishers advance regardless of party). [(CalMatters — SPI voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/superintendent-of-public-instruction/) [(LAist — SPI race explainer)](https://laist.com/news/politics/voter-guides/2026-election-california-primary-superintendent-public-instruction)

Richard Barrera, 59, is a San Diego native and son of a Colombian immigrant. He holds a bachelor's degree from UC San Diego and a master's in public policy from Harvard. He has served on the San Diego Unified School District board for 18 years (the state's second-largest district, ~103,000 students), five of them as board president, and has been a longtime union organizer and labor leader — serving as secretary-treasurer of the San Diego & Imperial Counties Labor Council (135 unions) while on the board. Since 2024 he has also worked as Senior Adviser for Special Projects at the CDE under Superintendent Thurmond. [(EdSource — Meet the candidates: Richard Barrera)](https://edsource.org/2026/richard-barrera-california-education-superintendent/756001) [(CA SoS — official candidate statement)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/superintendent-candidate-statements.htm)

## Record / past performance

Eighteen years on the San Diego Unified board (five as president), described in profiles as the district's "moral compass." His official statement claims San Diego now ranks "Number 1 in Reading and Math" among large urban districts on the National Assessment of Educational Progress and first in California across reading, math, science, graduation, and college/career readiness — these are campaign self-characterizations (Tier A as his claim, not independently verified here). [(CA SoS candidate statement)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/superintendent-candidate-statements.htm) Independently reported record per EdSource: early adoption of community schools; added college-prep ("A–G") courses in low-income high schools, with Latino students meeting UC/CSU standards reportedly rising from ~25% to ~70% within a decade of the 2009 adoption; mandated high-school ethnic studies; expanded bilingual hiring; negotiated above-average teacher compensation (a ~40–50% salary increase over roughly a dozen years; 2024–25 average ~$105,672) and full family health coverage; and ran the district on the minimum state-required 2% reserve. [(EdSource)](https://edsource.org/2026/richard-barrera-california-education-superintendent/756001) Since 2024 at the CDE he has worked on protecting immigrant students/families, district-built subsidized housing (San Diego pursuing ~3,000 units on district land), and reducing chronic absenteeism via "communities of practice." [(EdSource)](https://edsource.org/2026/richard-barrera-california-education-superintendent/756001)

## Endorsements & donors

**Endorsements.** California Teachers Association (CTA, ~300,000+ members) — the central endorsement; no SPI has been elected in ~44 years without CTA backing. [(EdSource)](https://edsource.org/2026/richard-barrera-california-education-superintendent/756001) [(CTA — Elect Richard Barrera)](https://www.cta.org/our-advocacy/elect-richard-barrera-for-california-superintendent-of-public-instruction) The California Charter Schools Association (CCSA) also endorsed him — a notable cross-coalition alignment given CTA and CCSA spent tens of millions opposing each other in 2018. [(CalMatters)](https://calmatters.org/newsletter/superintendent-california-election-endorsements/) Additional: United Administrators of Southern California (principals), Cindy Marten (former San Diego superintendent and Biden Deputy Secretary of Education), Superintendent Tony Thurmond, and editorial boards including the San Francisco Chronicle and McClatchy's Sacramento/Fresno/Merced Bees. [(CA SoS candidate statement)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/superintendent-candidate-statements.htm) [(EdSource)](https://edsource.org/2026/richard-barrera-california-education-superintendent/756001)

**Money — the CTA independent expenditure (confirmed).** The ~$5M push is an **independent expenditure (IE)**, not direct campaign money. CTA's IE committee spent more than $5 million supporting Barrera over the final ~six weeks of the race (of roughly $5.6M total IE spending across the contest). CTA President David Goldberg: "We are planning to spend as much as it takes for him to win." By contrast, Barrera's own campaign raised only ~$220,000 (fourth among major candidates). CCSA separately spent ~$40,000 on TV/web ads supporting him. [(EdSource — CTA pours $5M into Barrera)](https://edsource.org/updates/california-teachers-association-pours-5-million-into-barrera-campaign-in-final-leg-of-race) [(Voice of San Diego — unions drop $5M on Barrera)](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2026/05/27/the-learning-curve-unions-drop-5-million-on-barrera-for-state-supe/)

A voter wary of CTA/CFT capture should read Barrera as the most union-aligned viable candidate in the field. (inference, grounded in the sourced facts above.)

# Al Muratsuchi — 2026 CA Superintendent of Public Instruction candidate

## Background

Al Muratsuchi, 61, of Torrance (South Bay, Los Angeles County), is a Democratic State Assemblymember and chair of the Assembly Education Committee. He has served in the Assembly for ~12 years (with one term out, 2014–2016) and is termed out at the end of 2026. [(EdSource — Meet the candidates: Muratsuchi)](https://edsource.org/2026/meet-the-california-superintendent-candidates-al-muratsuchi/755784) Before the Legislature he was a civil-rights lawyer and California deputy attorney general, served on the Torrance Unified School District Board of Education, and chaired the Southern California Regional Occupation Center. He teaches government as an adjunct professor at El Camino Community College and is a member of American Federation of Teachers Local 1388. [(alforcaliforniaschools.com — Meet Al)](https://www.alforcaliforniaschools.com/meet-al) Born and raised on U.S. military bases in Okinawa, he moved to California in 1982; he graduated from UC Berkeley and earned a J.D. from UCLA. He describes himself in his ballot statement as "the son of immigrants, product of public schools, and a first generation college graduate." [(CA SoS — candidate statements)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/superintendent-candidate-statements.htm)

The office is nonpartisan; Muratsuchi is a Democrat (partisan lean: mainstream-labor Democrat, closely aligned with teacher/school-employee unions).

**About the office:** The Superintendent of Public Instruction (SPI) is California's nonpartisan elected head of K-12 education and runs the California Department of Education, overseeing roughly 6 million public-school students and a ~$150 billion education budget. Its authority is limited: local school districts "largely control budget and curriculum decisions," the Governor-appointed State Board of Education sets much statewide K-12 policy, and the Governor controls the budget — and the state is considering shifting further responsibility away from the SPI toward the State Board. The SPI's real levers are the statewide platform/bully pulpit, curriculum frameworks, instructional-materials adoption, and assessment/literacy initiatives. [(LAist — SPI race explainer)](https://laist.com/news/politics/voter-guides/2026-election-california-primary-superintendent-public-instruction) [(CalMatters — SPI voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/superintendent-of-public-instruction/) The seat is open: incumbent Tony Thurmond is termed out and ran for Governor. The field is broad — major contenders include Muratsuchi, former Assembly Speaker Anthony Rendon, San Diego Unified board president Richard Barrera (CTA-backed), former Sen. Josh Newman, LACCD trustee Nichelle Henderson, and conservative Chino Valley Unified board president Sonja Shaw. Top two advance to November. [(CalMatters — Who will break out)](https://calmatters.org/education/k-12-education/2026/05/ca-superintendent-election-2026/)

Campaign finance: Muratsuchi is second in the field with "more than $648,575" in contributions, his biggest contributors being unions and education-employee organizations — SEIU ($41,500), ILWU ($20,400), California School Employees Association PAC ($19,600), California Teachers Association ($18,115), and the Association of California School Administrators ($9,800); the California Federation of Teachers contributed $33,900. [(EdSource — Muratsuchi profile)](https://edsource.org/2026/meet-the-california-superintendent-candidates-al-muratsuchi/755784) He raised ~$61,000 in the April 18–May 16, 2026 window. [(EdSource — campaign finance)](https://edsource.org/2026/campaign-finance-california-superintendent/759476) These figures are reported by EdSource from disclosure filings (treat as filing-based).

**EDUCATION (lead axis):**

- **Science of reading / phonics:** This is Muratsuchi's strongest concrete curriculum credential and cuts toward a science-of-reading voter. In his ballot statement: "To improve reading test scores, I coauthored a law and delivered funding for more phonics-based reading instruction based on the science of reading." [(CA SoS)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/superintendent-candidate-statements.htm) The law is AB 1454 (2025), co-sponsored by Speaker Robert Rivas, Asm. Blanca Rubio, and Muratsuchi — it requires the state to provide teacher training and instructional materials for phonics-focused, structured-literacy instruction (the final compromise made district participation optional rather than mandatory; CTA, which had fought earlier mandates, went neutral). [(CalMatters — phonics fight)](https://calmatters.org/education/k-12-education/2025/04/phonics-california-reading/) Reform/literacy advocates (EdVoice's Marshall Tuck) backed the compromise. This is a real, recent, sourced science-of-reading win for Muratsuchi.
- **Charter schools / school choice:** Muratsuchi's record is charter-skeptical / oversight-oriented — the most relevant signal for a pro-charter voter, and it cuts against. He authored AB 84 (2025), a charter-oversight bill sponsored by the CTA that would have capped enrollment of nonclassroom-based charters authorized by small (<10,000-ADA) districts and created a statewide Office of the Education Inspector General with subpoena power and expanded charter audits. It was backed by the California School Boards Association and opposed by the California Charter Schools Association (CCSA) and the Charter Schools Development Center, who viewed it as a union effort "to chip away at charter schools." Muratsuchi shelved AB 84 days before the session ended amid the standoff, saying "Everything is on the table for negotiations… the most important thing… is for the governor to bring all stakeholders together." [(EdSource — charter reform negotiations)](https://edsource.org/2025/charter-school-reform-negotiations/746949) No pro-charter-expansion or school-choice position surfaced; his charter posture is regulation/accountability, aligned with the teacher unions. ## Record / past performance

**Education-policy depth (his strongest qualification).** Muratsuchi is the candidate most directly steeped in K-12 statute: chair of the Assembly Education Committee and of the budget subcommittee on education. He pitches himself as "the only candidate that has that combination of all three" — school-board member, legislator, and educator. [(EdSource)](https://edsource.org/2026/meet-the-california-superintendent-candidates-al-muratsuchi/755784) Concrete authored/co-authored wins: Proposition 2 (2024, $10B school bond, voter-approved), AB 1454 (2025, phonics/science-of-reading funding and training). [(CA SoS)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/superintendent-candidate-statements.htm) [(CalMatters — phonics fight)](https://calmatters.org/education/k-12-education/2025/04/phonics-california-reading/)

**Charter-oversight record.** AB 84 (2025) — CTA-sponsored, CCSA-opposed charter-oversight bill capping small-district nonclassroom-based charters and creating an inspector general — which he authored and then withdrew amid a stalemate. The rival charter-backed bill (SB 414, Sen. Ashby) passed but was vetoed by Newsom as too costly. [(EdSource — charter reform negotiations)](https://edsource.org/2025/charter-school-reform-negotiations/746949) This is the clearest signal that on charters Muratsuchi is aligned with the teacher unions' accountability/restriction camp, not with charter expansion. (inference on net orientation)

**Administrative competence.** Like Rendon, Muratsuchi's experience is legislative/advocacy plus a local school board and adjunct teaching — he has not run an agency the size of the CDE. His school-board tenure (Torrance Unified) and State Allocation Board service are his closest executive/governance experience. [(alforcaliforniaschools.com — Meet Al)](https://www.alforcaliforniaschools.com/meet-al) (inference: deep policy fluency, limited large-agency management experience.) **DATA-GAP** [axis: agency-management; severity: low; last-attempt: 2026-06-02].

**Viability.** Open question; currently mid-pack. The PPIC poll (March 26–April 3, 2026; 1,604 likely voters; ±3.2) had Muratsuchi at 6%, behind Rendon and Ainye Long (9% each) and Barrera/Shaw (~7%), tied with Newman, with 32% undecided and no candidate above 9%. [(EdSource — poll)](https://edsource.org/2026/california-voter-concerns-public-schools/756039) His assets are the CFT/CSEA institutional machine (he touts "more than 370,000 teachers and school staff" endorsing him) and genuine education-policy credibility; his liabilities are a fundraising deficit to Rendon (~$0.6M vs ~$1.2M) and a divided Democratic field. An analyst pegged $15–20M as the real cost of winning — above any candidate's haul — and warned a split Democratic vote could let conservative Sonja Shaw take a top-two slot.

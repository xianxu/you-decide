# Anthony Rendon — 2026 CA Superintendent of Public Instruction candidate

## Background

Anthony Rendon, 58, of Lakewood (southeast Los Angeles County), is a former Speaker of the California State Assembly. He served in the Assembly from 2012 and was Speaker from 2016 to 2023 — the second-longest Speaker tenure in state history after Willie Brown. [(EdSource — Meet the candidates: Rendon)](https://edsource.org/2026/anthony-rendon-california-superintendent/756021) Before politics he spent roughly two decades administering nonprofit preschool and early-childhood-education programs, including as executive director of Plaza de la Raza (overseeing 2,300+ children across 35 centers per his campaign). [(anthonyrendon.org — About)](https://www.anthonyrendon.org/about) He earned an associate degree from Cerritos (community) College, a B.A. and M.A. from Cal State Fullerton, and a Ph.D. in political science from UC Riverside (via a National Endowment for the Humanities fellowship), with postgraduate work at Boston University. [(EdSource)](https://edsource.org/2026/anthony-rendon-california-superintendent/756021) In his official ballot statement he describes himself as "the grandson of Mexican immigrants" who "barely graduated" high school before finding his footing at community college. [(CA SoS — candidate statements)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/superintendent-candidate-statements.htm)

The office is nonpartisan; Rendon is a Democrat (partisan lean: progressive/labor-aligned Democrat — he was the Democratic Assembly Speaker).

**About the office:** The Superintendent of Public Instruction (SPI) is California's nonpartisan elected head of K-12 education and runs the California Department of Education, overseeing a system of roughly 6 million public-school students and a ~$150 billion education budget. Its authority is limited: local school districts "largely control budget and curriculum decisions," the Governor-appointed State Board of Education sets much statewide K-12 policy, and the Governor controls the budget — and the state is actively considering shifting further responsibility away from the SPI toward the State Board. The SPI's real levers are the statewide platform/bully pulpit, curriculum frameworks, instructional-materials adoption, and assessment/literacy initiatives. [(LAist — SPI race explainer)](https://laist.com/news/politics/voter-guides/2026-election-california-primary-superintendent-public-instruction) [(CalMatters — SPI voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/superintendent-of-public-instruction/) The seat is open: incumbent Tony Thurmond is termed out and ran for Governor. The field is broad — major contenders include Rendon, Assemblymember Al Muratsuchi (CFT-backed), San Diego Unified board president Richard Barrera (CTA-backed), former Sen. Josh Newman, LACCD trustee Nichelle Henderson, and conservative Chino Valley Unified board president Sonja Shaw. Top two advance to November. [(CalMatters — Who will break out)](https://calmatters.org/education/k-12-education/2026/05/ca-superintendent-election-2026/)

Campaign finance: Rendon leads the field, reported at "nearly $1.2 million," but the bulk is carryover — roughly $1.1M transferred from an aborted run for State Treasurer. He raised "nearly $155,000" in new money in the April 18–May 16, 2026 filing window (a 36% increase), adding 37 new donors. [(EdSource — campaign finance)](https://edsource.org/2026/campaign-finance-california-superintendent/759476) These figures are reported by EdSource from disclosure filings (treat as filing-based, not aggregator).

## Record / past performance

**Legislative leadership.** Rendon was a powerful Speaker (2016–2023). A political scientist quoted by EdSource (Wesley Hussey) credited him with using power effectively and dispersing it among committee chairs, calling his run "pretty successful" — but noted Rendon "did not appear to play a leading role in shaping education policies during his tenure." [(EdSource)](https://edsource.org/2026/anthony-rendon-california-superintendent/756021) This is the central competence question: he has top-tier legislative/political-leadership experience but ran the chamber, not an education agency — no administrative experience running a large department like the CDE. (inference: legislative-leader, not administrator)

**Charter accountability era.** Rendon's campaign and CalMatters credit his Speaker tenure with shepherding "charter school accountability"/transparency legislation. [(anthonyrendon.org — About)](https://www.anthonyrendon.org/about) [(CalMatters — SPI voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/superintendent-of-public-instruction/) The landmark charter-restriction law of that period, AB 1505 (2019), was authored by Asm. Patrick O'Donnell and signed by Gov. Newsom; it ended State-Board charter authorization, let districts weigh fiscal impact, and tightened renewals. [(Gov. Newsom — charter signing 10.3.19)](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2019/10/03/governor-newsom-signs-charter-school-legislation-10-3-19/) [(EdSource — charter controls 2019)](https://edsource.org/2019/governor-lawmakers-agree-on-new-controls-on-california-charter-schools/616877) Rendon was Speaker when it passed and presided over a Democratic supermajority that enacted it; his campaign claims this lineage as his "education oversight" record. (inference: this is a Speaker-of-the-house association, not personal authorship of AB 1505 — corroborating personal authorship was not possible.) **DATA-GAP** [axis: personal-charter-vote; severity: low; last-attempt: 2026-06-02] — Rendon's individual floor votes on specific charter/curriculum bills were not retrieved from a Tier-A roll-call this pass.

**Budget/funding record.** Multiple Tier-B sources corroborate large K-12 funding increases and universal TK/universal pre-K during his Speakership. [(EdSource)](https://edsource.org/2026/anthony-rendon-california-superintendent/756021) [(CalMatters — SPI voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/superintendent-of-public-instruction/)

The PPIC poll (conducted March 26–April 3, 2026; 1,604 likely voters; ±3.2) found the race wide open: 32% undecided and no candidate above 9%. Rendon was at 9% (tied for the nominal lead with Ainye Long, who has not actively campaigned), with Barrera and Shaw ~7%, Muratsuchi and Newman ~6%, Henderson ~5%. ## Controversies / liabilities

- **Spouse's nonprofit/consulting income.** In 2021 the Sacramento Bee and the Center for Investigative Reporting documented that nonprofits connected to Rendon's wife, Annie Lam, received over $500,000 in donations and sponsorships from companies with business before the Legislature; Rendon said "There is no connection between any donations to Annie's nonprofits and what happens with respect to legislation." In 2023 the Los Angeles Times reported Lam earned ~$400,000 in 2022 from consulting for entities with legislative business; Lam said she stood "on my own abilities, accomplishments, and successes."

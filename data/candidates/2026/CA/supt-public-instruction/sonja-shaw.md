# Sonja Shaw — 2026 CA Superintendent of Public Instruction candidate

## Background

**About the office.** The Superintendent of Public Instruction (SPI) is California's nonpartisan elected head of K-12 education and runs the California Department of Education (CDE), overseeing schooling for nearly 6 million students against an ~$150 billion budget. The office has limited direct power: the Governor-appointed State Board of Education sets much policy, the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) leaves most budget and curriculum decisions to local districts, and the Governor controls the overall budget. The SPI's real levers are curriculum frameworks, instructional-materials approval, statewide literacy/assessment initiatives, and the bully pulpit. The 2026 seat is open because incumbent Tony Thurmond ran for Governor; the June 2, 2026 primary is top-two (the two highest finishers advance regardless of party). [(CalMatters — SPI voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/superintendent-of-public-instruction/) [(LAist — SPI race explainer)](https://laist.com/news/politics/voter-guides/2026-election-california-primary-superintendent-public-instruction)

Sonja Shaw, 43, is president of the Chino Valley Unified School District board, first elected in 2022. She describes herself as a mother of two and a former stay-at-home parent married to a construction manager, with no college education ("did not attend college"). California voters have historically elected SPIs with advanced degrees, making her profile unusual. By her own account she had minimal involvement in education policy before the pandemic ("didn't even know what a school board was"); a difficult childhood — caregiving for siblings amid a parent's heroin addiction, time in foster care — informs her stated distrust of "systems that don't work." Her political entry began when she enrolled her daughters in a charter school in Rancho Cucamonga offering in-person pandemic learning, then organized parents to attend Chino Valley board meetings and ran for an open seat. ## Record / past performance

**Chino Valley parental-notification / "forced outing" policy — the decisive, contested record.** In 2023 Shaw's board (she presiding) passed a policy requiring staff to notify parents, with minimal exceptions, when a student requested to use a name, pronoun, or facility not matching their birth-certificate sex — even when disclosure could harm the student. This drew immediate state legal action:

- **Aug 28, 2023:** AG Rob Bonta secured a temporary restraining order; the San Bernardino Superior Court issued a preliminary injunction in October 2023. [(CA DOJ — Bonta press release)](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-files-motion-seeking-permanent-injunction-and-declaratory)
- **March 7, 2024:** The district voted to rescind the original policy in response to the injunction, replacing it with a modified version (Policy 5020.1). [(Liberty Justice Center — case page)](https://libertyjusticecenter.org/cases/california-v-chino-valley-unified-school-district/)
- **2024 (statewide):** California enacted the SAFETY Act, prohibiting forced-disclosure policies of this kind. [(EdSource)](https://edsource.org/2026/sonja-shaw-california-superintendent-candidate/756133)
- **Sept 11, 2024 — final ruling:** The San Bernardino Superior Court (in *People of the State of California v. Chino Valley Unified School District*) issued a permanent injunction barring the gender-identity-disclosure provisions, finding they violated California's Equal Protection Clause, Education Code §220, and Government Code §11135 by discriminating against transgender and gender-nonconforming students. The court separately **upheld** the portion (§1.c) requiring notice of changes to a student's official/unofficial records as constitutional. [(CA DOJ — final-ruling press release)](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-superior-courts-final-ruling-against-chino-valleys-forced)

Presented neutrally: Shaw and supporters frame this as a parental-rights win ("landmark battles against Sacramento's extreme policies"); the legal outcome is that the gender-disclosure provisions were permanently enjoined as unlawful discrimination, while a narrower records-notification provision survived. [(EdSource)](https://edsource.org/2026/sonja-shaw-california-superintendent-candidate/756133) [(CA DOJ)](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-superior-courts-final-ruling-against-chino-valleys-forced)

This is a campaign self-characterization; the dossier does not independently verify the magnitude or attribution of the gains.

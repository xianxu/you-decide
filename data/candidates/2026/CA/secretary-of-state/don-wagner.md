# Don Wagner — 2026 CA Secretary of State candidate

## Background

Don Wagner (Donald P. Wagner, born December 3, 1960, Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania) is a Republican and a member of the Orange County Board of Supervisors. He is an attorney (UCLA BA; UC Hastings JD, 1987) who founded the Orange County chapter of the Federalist Society. He is married to Orange County Superior Court Judge Megan Wagner. (Orienting bio — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_P._Wagner) and [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Donald_P._Wagner), orienting-only.)

This is the June 2, 2026 top-two primary, with the general on November 3, 2026. Wagner is the only notable Republican in a four-candidate field (incumbent Democrat Shirley Weber, Wagner, and two Green Party candidates); the top two advance. [(CalMatters race story)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/04/california-secretary-state-race/)

**About the office he seeks:** The California Secretary of State is the state's chief elections officer, but the office's work also spans business filings, campaign-finance and lobbying disclosure (Cal-Access), and the state archives. The office has limited independent policy power: the election reforms Wagner campaigns on — voter ID, an Election-Day mail-ballot deadline, restrictions on "ballot harvesting," and rolling back universal vote-by-mail — generally require action by the Legislature, not a unilateral decision by the Secretary. [(CalMatters voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/secretary-of-state/) [(LAist voter guide)](https://laist.com/news/politics/voter-guides/2026-election-california-primary-secretary-of-state) Wagner's own platform leans on "common-sense reforms" that, in practice, would depend on the Legislature. ## Controversies / liabilities

**1. Andrew Do episode (most substantive) — with a mitigating turn:**
In January 2024, Wagner opposed a reform that would have required Orange County supervisors to disclose contracts awarded to family members, downplaying concerns about fellow Supervisor Andrew Do's grant: "There are no, nor should there be, questions or challenges as to that particular grant of money." The reform deadlocked 2–2. Do was later sentenced to five years in federal prison for an roughly $800,000 bribery scheme that steered more than $12 million in contracts. [(Voice of OC, Jan 2024)](https://voiceofoc.org/2024/01/oc-supervisors-decide-against-disclosing-family-contracts/)
**Mitigating turn:** By September 2024, as Board Chair, Wagner moved to strip Do of all committee assignments. [(LAist — OC supervisors Andrew Do vote)](https://laist.com/news/politics/oc-supervisors-andrew-do-vote) (Both are presented together.)

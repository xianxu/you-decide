# Michael Feinstein — 2026 CA Secretary of State candidate

## Background

Michael Feinstein is a Green Party candidate, a former Santa Monica city official, and a longtime electoral-reform advocate. He served on the Santa Monica City Council for two four-year terms (1996–2004) and as Mayor of Santa Monica (2000–2002). He co-founded the Green Party of California, playing an "integral role in the successful 1990-1991 ballot drive of the Green Party of California," and has long worked to "transform the electoral system to a system of proportional representation with public financing." He has served as a delegate to the Global Greens and the Federation of Green Parties of the Americas. [(GPCA spokesperson bio)](https://www.cagreens.org/spokespeople/feinstein) His ballot designation in this race is "Electoral Reform Consultant." [(LAist voter guide)](https://laist.com/news/politics/voter-guides/2026-election-california-primary-secretary-of-state)

He was born in Greece (adopted by American parents and brought to the United States at about four months old), raised in St. Louis Park, Minnesota, and earned a BA in philosophy from Carleton College (1982). (Orienting bio — [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Feinstein_(California)), orienting-only.)

This is the June 2, 2026 top-two primary, with the general on November 3, 2026. The field has four candidates: incumbent Democrat Shirley Weber, Republican Don Wagner, and two Green Party candidates — Feinstein and Gary Blenner. The top two advance. [(LAist voter guide)](https://laist.com/news/politics/voter-guides/2026-election-california-primary-secretary-of-state) Note that the two Greens are running against each other; the statewide Green Party of California endorsed Blenner (not Feinstein) on its "Left Unity Slate," and Feinstein carries a Los Angeles County Green Party endorsement rather than the GPCA statewide endorsement (see Endorsements). [(GPCA — Greens in June 2026)](https://cagreens.org/elections/greens_june_2026)

**About the office he seeks:** The California Secretary of State is the state's chief elections officer — the office "oversees the administration of all federal and state elections in California, including certifying candidates and initiatives for statewide ballots, issuing voter guides and ensuring votes are properly counted" — and its work also spans corporate business filings, campaign-finance and lobbying disclosure, and the state archives. The office has limited independent policy power: the structural reforms at the center of Feinstein's platform — proportional representation and ranked-choice voting — are not something the Secretary can adopt unilaterally; they would require the Legislature or statewide ballot measures. [(CalMatters voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/secretary-of-state/) [(LAist voter guide)](https://laist.com/news/politics/voter-guides/2026-election-california-primary-secretary-of-state) The transparency and administration items he proposes are closer to the office's actual levers, but his marquee proportional-representation agenda depends on the Legislature or the ballot. ## Stated positions

Feinstein frames his campaign as "A Vote for Michael Feinstein for Secretary of State is a Vote for a Democracy Bill of Rights for California." [(feinstein4sos.org)](https://feinstein4sos.org/) The platform planks below are drawn from his official voter-guide candidate statement (Tier A) and his campaign site.

**Electoral structure (his signature issue):**
- Proportional representation — "proportional representation elections—where like-minded voters receive representation in proportion to their numbers." [(SoS official candidate statement)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/sos-candidate-statements.htm)
- Ranked-choice voting — "Empower voters with more voice over more candidates, positive campaigning, and coalition-building." [(SoS official candidate statement)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/sos-candidate-statements.htm)
- He has carried this message directly to local bodies during the campaign, presenting on "proportional representation and voting rights" to city councils (e.g., Pomona, May 2026) and announcing appearances before San Diego–area councils framed around "multi-seat legislative districts by proportional representation," a response to the weakening of the federal Voting Rights Act and a critique of California's top-two "jungle primary." [(GPCA — Feinstein for SoS)](https://www.gp.org/feinstein_for_california_sos) [(feinstein4sos.org press advisory, May 15 2026)](https://feinstein4sos.org/press-advisory-2026-05-15/)
- Caveat: proportional representation and ranked-choice voting require the Legislature or a ballot measure, not a unilateral act by the Secretary of State. (inference)

**Election administration / transparency:**
- "publicly-owned, open-source voting equipment, paper ballots, and meaningful audits." [(SoS official candidate statement)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/sos-candidate-statements.htm)

**Voting access:**
- "Universal voter registration. Expand language access. Make it easier to vote by declaring election days state holidays." [(SoS official candidate statement)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/sos-candidate-statements.htm)

**Campaign finance:**
- "Stop the corrupting influence of Big Money"; "Match small donor contributions with public funds. Empower residents with democracy vouchers." [(SoS official candidate statement)](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/sos-candidate-statements.htm)

**Election legitimacy:** Feinstein's platform is pro-transparency, pro-audit, and pro-access — the opposite posture from election denialism. No "2020 was stolen" or fraud claims surfaced; his framing is reform-and-transparency oriented (paper ballots, meaningful audits, open-source equipment).

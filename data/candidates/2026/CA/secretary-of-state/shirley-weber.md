# Shirley Weber — 2026 CA Secretary of State candidate

## Background

Shirley Weber is a Democrat and California's Secretary of State, the incumbent in this race. She was appointed to the office by Governor Gavin Newsom on December 22, 2020 and sworn in on January 29, 2021, becoming the first Black Californian to serve as Secretary of State. [(CalMatters race story)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/04/california-secretary-state-race/) Before the appointment she served eight years in the California State Assembly and was a longtime professor at San Diego State University; she is the daughter of Arkansas sharecroppers. (Orienting bio — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Weber), orienting-only.)

She won a full term in 2022 with 60.1% of the vote (roughly 6.46 million votes) against Republican Robert Bernosky, and is eligible to seek a second term with no term-limit bar. [(CalMatters race story)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/04/california-secretary-state-race/) Her daughter, Akilah Weber, won an Assembly seat in 2021 and a State Senate seat in 2024 — both by election rather than appointment. (Orienting bio — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Weber), orienting-only.)

This is the June 2, 2026 top-two primary, with the general on November 3, 2026. Four candidates are on the ballot: Weber (D, incumbent), Don Wagner (R), and two Green Party candidates (Feinstein and Blenner). The top two advance. [(CalMatters race story)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/04/california-secretary-state-race/)

**About the office she seeks:** The California Secretary of State is the state's chief elections officer, but the office's day-to-day work is broader than elections — it also handles business filings, campaign-finance and lobbying disclosure (the Cal-Access system), and the state archives. The office has limited independent policy power: many of the election "reforms" candidates campaign on (voter ID, changing vote-by-mail rules, changing the canvass timeline) require action by the Legislature, not a unilateral decision by the Secretary. [(CalMatters voter guide)](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/secretary-of-state/) [(LAist voter guide)](https://laist.com/news/politics/voter-guides/2026-election-california-primary-secretary-of-state) Weber's stated second-term agenda is therefore mostly administrative (access, transparency, cybersecurity, vote-by-mail, filing modernization) rather than legislative. ## Stated positions

Sources below draw on her official campaign priorities page (primary), official Secretary of State releases and process pages (primary), and the nonpartisan CalMatters and LAist voter guides.

**Voter access:**
- Expand access through rural outreach, high-school and college registration drives, and voter education for the formerly incarcerated following Proposition 17. [(campaign priorities)](https://drshirleyweber.com/priorities/)
- Defend permanent universal vote-by-mail as a feature, not a problem to be rolled back. [(campaign priorities)](https://drshirleyweber.com/priorities/) [(CalMatters race story)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/04/california-secretary-state-race/)

**Election security / cybersecurity:**
- Names election cybersecurity a top second-term priority. [(campaign priorities)](https://drshirleyweber.com/priorities/) The office runs an Election Security Program with tabletop drills and county trainings alongside state and federal partners. [(SoS — election cybersecurity)](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/election-cybersecurity)

**Voter ID — opposes:**
- Weber opposes voter-ID requirements. With Attorney General Rob Bonta she filed suit (April 15, 2024) challenging a Huntington Beach local voter-ID charter amendment, calling it "a solution in search of a problem" and stating that "Voter ID requirements have historically been used to turn eligible voters away." [(SoS news release, Apr 2024)](https://www.sos.ca.gov/administration/news-releases-and-advisories/2024-news-releases-and-advisories/secretary-state-shirley-n-weber-phd-and-attorney-general-rob-bonta-file-lawsuit-challenge-voter-id-law)

**On counting speed vs. accuracy:**
- Weber frames the trade-off explicitly toward accuracy: "I know the value of being fast for some folks. For me, accuracy is far more important." [(Santa Barbara Independent, May 4 2026)](https://www.independent.com/2026/05/04/californias-race-for-secretary-of-state-shows-partisan-divide-over-how-to-count-ballots/)
- The slow ballot count is statutory, not within her discretion: state law gives counties up to 30 days to canvass results. [(SoS — vote counting process)](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/upcoming-elections/vote-counting-process) The drivers are mailing every voter a ballot, counting mail ballots that are postmarked by Election Day and received within seven days, per-ballot signature verification with a voter cure window, and same-day/conditional registration producing provisional ballots. [(CapRadio, May 27 2026)](https://www.capradio.org/articles/2026/05/27/why-timing-postmarks-and-signatures-matter-for-californias-vote-by-mail-majority/) A CalMatters commentary attributes the slowness to ballot volume and processing design rather than to mailed votes per se. [(CalMatters commentary, Apr 2026)](https://calmatters.org/commentary/2026/04/slow-vote-count-california-ballots/)

**Cal-Access / CARS modernization — weak area, largely inherited:**
- Cal-Access, the campaign-finance disclosure system, is antiquated and suffers frequent outages. [(CalMatters, Apr 2024)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2024/04/california-campaign-finance-website/)
- The CARS replacement project started in 2016 under predecessor Alex Padilla. The first build (rolled out June 2021) was scrapped as architecturally flawed; Weber paused and then restarted the project. Roughly $70 million was allocated, with at least $40 million spent by September 2022 and $16.8 million more sought in 2024–25; vendor CGI was selected in 2024, with the new system expected mid-November 2026 — after the 2026 election. [(CalMatters, Apr 2024)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2024/04/california-campaign-finance-website/) [(SoS — CARS updates)](https://www.sos.ca.gov/campaign-lobbying/helpful-resources/cal-access-replacement-system-project-cars-updates)

## Controversies / liabilities

**1. Cal-Access / CARS modernization delays (most substantive):**
The state's campaign-finance disclosure infrastructure remains antiquated and outage-prone, and the long-running CARS replacement — begun under her predecessor in 2016, with a scrapped first build and tens of millions spent — is not expected to go live until after the 2026 election. Much of this is inherited, but transparency-and-disclosure modernization is squarely within the office's mission. [(CalMatters, Apr 2024)](https://calmatters.org/politics/2024/04/california-campaign-finance-website/) [(SoS — CARS updates)](https://www.sos.ca.gov/campaign-lobbying/helpful-resources/cal-access-replacement-system-project-cars-updates)

**2. Slow ballot count (largely not her discretion):**
California's multi-week count draws partisan criticism, but the timeline is set by statute (30-day county canvass) and by design choices (universal mail, postmark-plus-seven-days acceptance, signature verification, provisional ballots) rather than by the Secretary's discretion. Weber defends accuracy over speed. [(SoS — vote counting process)](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/upcoming-elections/vote-counting-process) [(CapRadio, May 27 2026)](https://www.capradio.org/articles/2026/05/27/why-timing-postmarks-and-signatures-matter-for-californias-vote-by-mail-majority/) [(Santa Barbara Independent, May 4 2026)](https://www.independent.com/2026/05/04/californias-race-for-secretary-of-state-shows-partisan-divide-over-how-to-count-ballots/)

## Measure RTM

**Yes:** Impose a 14-year sales/use tax: 0.5 percentage points in San Mateo, Alameda, Contra Costa and Santa Clara counties; 1 point in San Francisco. **No:** Reject this additional tax. Approval requires a majority across the five-county district, not separate majorities in each county. Estimated revenue is $980 million annually. In San Mateo, this adds $5 to a $1,000 taxable purchase (arithmetic illustration). [Official analysis](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-M206-ImpartialAnalysis-Public-Transit-District-Sales-Tax.pdf)

Funding supports transit operations, integration/accessibility and eligible road repaving; distributions vary by county. The analysis describes financial-efficiency reviews, implementation plans, potential withholding for noncompliance and independent distribution oversight. Revenue is an estimate, not a guaranteed collection. [Official analysis](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-M206-ImpartialAnalysis-Public-Transit-District-Sales-Tax.pdf)

**For:** The filed Yes argument says funding would avert major Caltrain/BART service cuts, preserve access for transit-dependent residents and reduce congestion; its specific closure scenarios are advocacy forecasts. [Argument in favor](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-M206-Contest%20Code-4ArginFavor-Public%20Transit%20District%20Sales%20Tax_Redacted_0.pdf)

**Against:** The filed No argument, signed among others by Silicon Valley Taxpayers Association president Mark Hinkle, calls for management reform before new funding, criticizes sales-tax regressivity and questions oversight and cross-county allocations. Its management allegations are not adjudicated here. [Argument against](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-M206-Contest%20Code-46ArgAgainst-Public%20Transit%20District%20Sales%20Tax_Redacted.pdf)

## Measure V

**Yes:** Authorize up to $848 million in San Mateo County Community College District facilities bonds. **No:** Deny this new borrowing authority. Passage requires 55%. The impartial analysis estimates $1.35 billion total repayment, including interest, through June 2049; the estimated highest annual levy is $18 per $100,000 assessed value. A $1 million assessment implies approximately $180 annually at that rate, not at market value. [Official analysis](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-MV-ImpartialAnalysis-SMCCD-Bond.pdf)

Projects include building systems, safety, technology and facilities improvements at district campuses. Oversight and annual audits are required; proceeds generally cannot fund ordinary salaries/operations. Project listing does not establish priority. [Official analysis](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-MV-ImpartialAnalysis-SMCCD-Bond.pdf)

**For:** The filed supporters argue aging campuses and career-training facilities need repairs and modernization, while financing can continue the current tax rate. [Argument in favor](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-M307-Contest%20Code-5ArgInFavor-SMCCD%20Bond_Redacted.pdf)

**Against:** The filed opposition challenges previous spending and proposed project priorities and argues extending tax payments is a real cost even without a higher rate. Its specific accusations require independent verification. [Argument against](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-M307-Contest%20Code-6ArgAgainst-SMCCD%20Bond_Redacted.pdf)

**Uncertainty:** The tax statement estimates an average $10.50 per $100,000, with the $18 peak beginning 2045–46. Existing debt's retirement underlies the no-rate-increase projection. Estimates are not binding caps: issuance, interest and assessed-value growth affect actual bills. No rate increase does not mean no new debt. [Tax-rate statement, pages 15–16](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-M307-Contest%20Code-Reso-SMCCD%20Bond_Redacted.pdf)

## Measure J

**Yes:** Extend from 30 to 60 days the Board of Supervisors' deadline to appoint someone or order a special election after a vacancy in another elected county office. Require at least one noticed public meeting for comment on a proposed appointment and candidates' qualifications. **No:** Retain the 30-day rule without this express appointment-hearing requirement. It does not cover vacancies on the Board itself. Majority approval is required. [Official analysis](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-MJ-ImpartialAnalysis-SMC-Charter-Extension-to-Call%20Special%20Election.pdf)

If the Board misses the revised deadline, the elections officer must still call a special election. Other special-election timing and resignation procedures remain unchanged. No sunset or tax authorization is described; the analysis gives no quantified fiscal effect. [Official analysis](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-MJ-ImpartialAnalysis-SMC-Charter-Extension-to-Call%20Special%20Election.pdf)

**Arguments and uncertainty:** No filed argument for or against appears on the county page. An analytical tradeoff, not an attributed campaign claim, is more time and mandatory public input versus a longer permitted vacancy-resolution period. Any election-cost savings would depend on future appointments and are not established by this research. [County election materials](https://smcacre.gov/elections/november-3-2026-statewide-general-election)

## Measure U

**Yes:** Require an independent commission, governed by applicable California election law, to redraw San Mateo County supervisorial districts after each federal census. **No:** Retain the Board's authority to adopt district boundaries. Majority approval is required. The amendment preserves district elections, residency requirements and existing supervisor term limits. This is the **2026 county charter measure**, distinct from the MPCSD school bond called Measure U in 2024. [Official analysis](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-MU-ImpartialAnalysis-SMC-Charter-Reapportionment.pdf); [2024 bond audit](https://district-mpcsd.community.highbond.com/document/432819cc-bf9f-48e4-9494-36d73d6ec83f/)

**Costs and duration:** An ongoing charter rule applying after each decennial census; no sunset or new tax is specified. The analysis supplies no quantified cost for staffing or operating the commission. State-law independence restrictions are part of the framework, but do not guarantee any particular electoral outcome. [Official analysis](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-MU-ImpartialAnalysis-SMC-Charter-Reapportionment.pdf)

**For:** Filed proponents, including supervisors and League of Women Voters leaders, argue that transferring final authority reduces elected officials' conflicts and improves participation and public trust. These are anticipated benefits. [Argument in favor](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-204-Contest%20Code-5ArgInFavor-SMC%20Charter_Redacted.pdf)

**Against/gap:** No opposing ballot argument is listed. This should not be interpreted as unanimous public support; operating costs and implementation details remain questions to follow. [County election materials](https://smcacre.gov/elections/november-3-2026-statewide-general-election)

## Measure P

**Yes:** Require voter approval for specified changes to eight city-owned downtown parking plazas that diminish parking availability, access or convenience. **No:** Retain current council decision-making authority. Passage requires a majority. Qualifying actions include conveyances, surplus-land designation and parking-reducing changes. Exceptions cover parking-preserving improvements and temporary community events of up to three consecutive days. The rule reaches back to May 15, 2025; amendment or repeal requires another public vote. It has no sunset. [City attorney's analysis](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-MP-ImpartialAnalysis-MenloPark-Muni-Amendment.pdf)

**Costs:** It imposes no tax. The city's impact report identifies possible development delays and fiscal effects from additional voting requirements; actual costs depend on projects, elections and litigation, rather than a fixed amount. [City impact report](https://www.menlopark.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/2/agendas-and-minutes/city-council/2025-meetings/20251202/f1-20251202-cc-receive-election-report-9212_.pdf)

**For:** Filed supporters, including former mayors Catherine Carlton and Robert McNamara, argue voters should decide before parking is converted. They fear business disruption and public garage costs, and favor affordable-housing alternatives. These are campaign claims, not independently validated forecasts. [Argument in favor](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-M102-ContestCode-7ArgInFavor-Menlo%20Park%20Muni%20Amendment_Redacted.pdf)

**Against:** Filed opponents argue it obstructs affordable housing and downtown renewal and risks costly state-law conflicts. These predicted consequences are advocacy claims, not established court outcomes. [Argument against](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-M102-Contest%20Code-6ArgAgainst-Menlo%20Park%20Muni%20Amendment_Redacted_0.pdf)

**Key distinction:** The legal mechanism is an additional voter-approval condition, not an unconditional ban on all downtown housing. The analysis specifically identifies implications for the city's 345-unit affordable-housing program. [Official analysis](https://smcacre.gov/system/files/2026-08/52-ENG-MP-ImpartialAnalysis-MenloPark-Muni-Amendment.pdf)

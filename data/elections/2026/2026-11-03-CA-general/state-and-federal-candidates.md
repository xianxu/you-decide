## Governor — Steve Hilton (Republican) and Xavier Becerra (Democratic)

**Background and record.** Becerra previously served in Congress, as California attorney general, and as President Biden’s health secretary. Hilton advised UK Prime Minister David Cameron and later hosted a Fox News program; his executive experience is different from Becerra’s elected and agency leadership. [AP's nominee report](https://apnews.com/article/ead2e489977a95692300735520cae195)

**Current priorities.** Becerra proposes emergency action on insurance and utility prices, financing already-approved affordable homes, denser housing, and continued resistance to Trump administration policies. Hilton proposes less restrictive construction rules, more single-family starter homes, substantial tax cuts, and rollback of climate mandates. Both oppose the billionaire wealth-tax measure. The current guide reports Hilton’s income-tax exemption proposal as the first **$150,000**, superseding the $100,000 described in June reporting. These are proposals, not enacted benefits. [Current CalMatters comparison](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/governor/)

**Material record concern.** An HHS inspector-general review of early-2021 child placements found missing documentation of required sponsor checks and delayed follow-up. This supports scrutiny of agency management; it does not establish that every child not reached by phone was missing. [OIG report](https://www.oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2024/gaps-in-sponsor-screening-and-followup-raise-safety-concerns-for-unaccompanied-children/) Becerra disputes the characterization of children as “lost” and argues subsequent employer exploitation was outside his agency’s custody. [CalMatters reporting and response](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/05/california-governor-becerra-criticism/)

**Difference and uncertainty.** The clearest contrast is public financing/regulation versus tax reductions/deregulation. Neither label resolves implementation costs. Immigration positions also warrant care: September 30 reporting says Hilton avoided committing on repeal of California’s sanctuary law during the debate, despite stronger earlier positioning. [Debate report](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/09/california-general-election-gubernatorial-debate-cnn/) Fiscal feasibility and the legal reach of emergency price freezes remain unverified here.

## Secretary of state — Donald P. (Don) Wagner (Republican) and Shirley N. Weber (Democratic)

**Background and record.** Weber is the incumbent, appointed in 2021 and elected in 2022 after Assembly service. Her tenure includes permanent universal mail-ballot implementation. Wagner is an Orange County supervisor, former Irvine mayor, and former assemblymember. He supports voter identification and pressed for sharing voter information with the federal Department of Justice. Weber emphasizes access, cybersecurity, and election-filing transparency. [CalMatters general-election profiles](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/secretary-of-state/)

**Stated priorities.** Wagner promises faster counts, voter-roll maintenance, and a nonpartisan approach to administering elections. Weber promises to defend registration and ballot access, including mail voting, and protect election workers. Wagner’s claims that ballots are mailed improperly and Weber’s claims about opponents’ motives are campaign arguments; the candidate statements do not independently establish them. [November candidate statements](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/candidates/secretary-of-state-candidate-statements.htm)

**Key difference.** The dispute concerns which election safeguards to prioritize: Wagner emphasizes identification, roll accuracy, and speed; Weber emphasizes access and protection of existing voting methods. Faster reporting and accurate counting are separate metrics, so a promise of speed needs an explanation of treatment of late-arriving valid ballots and signature correction. This is an analytical distinction, not a claim that either candidate intends inaccurate counts.

**Evidence gaps.** This research did not independently audit county voter rolls, quantify errors, or verify that Wagner’s changes could be implemented by the secretary without legislation. It also did not independently measure improvements in cybersecurity under Weber. The available evidence supports comparing stated administration priorities, not a finding that California’s certified elections are fraudulent or that an identification requirement by itself establishes security.

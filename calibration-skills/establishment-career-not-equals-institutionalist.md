# Long career ≠ institutionalist virtue

The institutionalist axis (Tier 2) measures whether a candidate defends procedural and institutional norms when politically inconvenient — election results, court orders, due process, agency independence. A long career inside established institutions (Congress, AG office, cabinet) is a *necessary but not sufficient* signal. It indicates fluency with institutions, not willingness to defend them under pressure.

## Triggering case

Xavier Becerra was initially scored +2 on institutionalist based on his Congress → CA AG → HHS Secretary path. Codex peer review pushed back: "long career is not auto +2 — assess actual institutional defense behavior." That correction is right. Becerra's HHS migrant-children oversight failures and broader management/accountability concerns are themselves institutional-stewardship signals — they cut against a +2. Revised to +1.

## How to score the institutionalist axis going forward

- **+2** — Documented defense of institutional norms under pressure (refused election denial, fought for agency independence against own-party pressure, etc.)
- **+1** — Clean record without notable defense or violation; long career adds confidence but doesn't elevate further
- **0** — Career in institutions but unclear stewardship; default for "long-established but no salient signal"
- **−1** — Management/accountability concerns; agency dysfunction on their watch without correction; participated in expedient norm-bending (e.g., 2012 DNC voice vote)
- **−2** — Active institutional violation (election interference, ballot seizure, agency capture). Usually triggers a hard filter, not just a score

## When it applies

Any candidate being scored on the institutionalist axis — especially long-career establishment candidates where the temptation is to use career-length as proxy.

## Don't confuse with

- **Anti-personalist-strongman** (Tier 1) — that's character, not stewardship
- **Procedural-justice for judges** — closely related, but operationalized via jurisprudential method, not biography

# Abolish-X positions — score on dual axes

Candidates who advocate abolishing a federal or state agency (abolish ICE, defund police, eliminate Department of Education, etc.) take a position that simultaneously:

1. **Reduces government coercive reach** → aligns with *leave-people-alone* (Tier 2)
2. **Dismantles an institutional structure** → cuts against *institutionalist / procedural-justice* (Tier 2)

These are different axes pulling opposite directions. Score on both. Don't collapse into a single "interventionist" or "fiscal expansion" reading — that's the framing error Codex peer review caught.

## Triggering case

Katie Porter's abolish-ICE-adjacent positioning was initially framed as evidence of "state expansion" / fiscal-interventionism. Codex pushed back: "abolish-ICE is not state expansion in the ordinary sense; it is civil-liberties/immigration-left." That's right. The proper reads:

- Civil-liberties dimension → *leave-people-alone* axis gets a positive bump
- Agency-dismantling dimension → *institutionalist* axis gets a negative

The economic-platform critique of Porter stands separately on *anti-tax-and-spend*; don't double-count by also calling abolish-ICE "expansive."

## Score guidance

| Position shape | leave-people-alone | institutionalist |
|---|---|---|
| Strong abolish-rhetoric, no replacement plan | +1 | −2 |
| Abolish + replacement-institution plan | +1 | −1 |
| Reform-not-abolish | 0 | 0 |

## When it applies

- Any "abolish [agency]" position
- Any "defund [agency]" position with significant funding-elimination intent
- Wholesale-replacement plans for existing institutions

## Don't confuse with

- **Anti-tax-and-spend** — abolishing an agency could reduce spending (positive on that axis). Score independently.
- **Issue-specific position** (Tier 4) — abolish-framing is about method, not the policy goal. "Humane immigration enforcement" as a goal can pair with either "reform ICE" or "abolish ICE" as method; the method scores on Tier 2 axes.
